## Added (1)

1. **solve-wp22-g040: "enough for n, but not for n + 1" gives two inequalities.** This is the common real-exam wording; it comes up again in percent and price questions. The video solved it but never said the general pattern. Four sentences were added before the goodbye. Enough for six but not seven gives two inequalities: six tickets cost the money or less, and seven cost more. So the price is squeezed between two numbers. Whenever you see "enough for some number but not for one more", write both. Board: "enough for 6, not for 7:" and `6p ≤ 105 < 7p`, each written at its clause.
