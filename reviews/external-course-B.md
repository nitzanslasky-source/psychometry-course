# External course review — Group B (word problems: ratios, work, motion, percent, overlap, combinatorics)

21 Oct 2026. 17 transcripts were read in full. 3 of them are music only, so 14 have content. Each one was compared with our course
(t22, t23, t24, t26, t27, t28, plus t02/t07 where relevant) and with real-exam frequency (quant_real.md, 19 English sections).
Note: t27 and t28 are still the older studio versions.

Overall: our course already covers almost everything in these videos, often in more depth. Examples: the ratio table and
triangle value, the V method, worker-hours, compare by factors, the squares method, three-group min/max, glue / round table /
repeats. The findings below are mostly small additions (one-line tips), plus four real gaps.

Counts: **15 GAP (most of them PARTIAL), 3 BETTER, 3 CHECK.**

## 1. מבוא לבעיות לפסיכומטרי 1 [GWphBvWe7FA]
**Teaches:** turning sentences into variables, then into equations: ages, "times", "less than", "a fraction of", "costs the same as". Also the "grocery principle": price × number of items = total.

- `GAP (PARTIAL)` **Which unknown gets the letter.** Their rule: give the letter to the quantity that the others are described by (the one after "than" / "of"), because it does not depend on anyone. If that leads to a division ("3 times shorter than"), flip it and give the letter to the smaller one. We already have "let x be what they ask" (wp-037), "Ben is the smaller one" (wp-034) and "give it to the little guy" (wp-039). What we never say is the concrete reading rule: *the person after "than" gets the letter, and the other is written in terms of them.* Where: wp-037 (tip) or wp-027-2. Exam: almost every equation word problem. **ADD AS ONE-LINE TIP.**
- `GAP (PARTIAL)` **Name unknowns by their initials, not x/y.** For example, H for Hagit and S for Sarit. Then at stage 3 you can see at a glance whether the value you found is the one they asked about. We do this once (M and P for mugs and pens, wp-027-3) but never say why. It supports our "stage three: answer what they asked". **ADD AS ONE-LINE TIP** in wp-037.
- `GAP (PARTIAL)` **Price per item = total ÷ number of items, with letters** (a sweets for b credits → b/a each). We use this all the time (r26-t22-04, tickets in g040) but never state it once as a rule, and the letter form is where weak students freeze. Exam: letter-answer word problems are common (e.g. 2022_autumn_q2_09 is the motion version). **ADD AS ONE-LINE TIP** in r26-t22-04.

## 2. Introduction to Psychometric Problems 2 [YIUxWB8L7H8]
**Teaches:** their "double-D" method, which is our ratio table with the triangle value. They use it on percents (60% ↔ 420 → 40% ↔ ?), fractions, work and motion.

- `BETTER` **When to reach for the ratio table.** Our trigger (wp-028, g029/g030) is "same rate / steady pace". Their trigger is wider and easy to spot: *two numbers in the question that describe the same thing in two units*, such as a percent and its amount in shekels, a fraction and a count, or km and the hour inside a speed. They also show the "what's left" pairing: she threw away 4/5 of her shirts and 32 are left, so 1/5 ↔ 32, and the thrown-away 4/5 ↔ 128. For a weak student this cue is easier to see than "the rate is the same". Change: in wp-028 add one sentence: "Whenever two numbers describe the same thing, a percent and its amount, a fraction and a count, write them as one row." In wp-052 (percent), show the 60% ↔ 420 row once.

## 3. Introduction to Psychometric Exam Problems 3 [4ORGjKwJzds]
**Teaches:** mixed examples: an age equation, the ratio table with 2½, adding two equations to get the expression they ask for directly, a nested translation ("x is a quarter of the number that is 3 more than an eighth of y"), and a shaded-squares area.

- `GAP (one-line)` **Multiplying a product: multiply only one factor.** For example, 2 · ½(r+1): the 2 cancels the ½ only, and the brackets stay as they are. Students multiply both. This belongs in the equations topic (t06/t07, older versions). Not found in our course. **ADD AS ONE-LINE TIP** when t06/t07 are rewritten.
- Otherwise nothing new. We cover: adding equations for a target expression (t07 q-195, t22 g049 / g042), the 2.5-type division (t02), and the phrase translation (wp-027-2/3).

## 4. Ratios for the Psychometric Exam 1 [PTSrPriCemU]
**Teaches:** a ratio is a fraction, read in order. What a ratio tells you: divisibility, part : total, part : difference. A ratio alone gives no amounts. Amounts as 3x and 5x. The ratio table with ratio numbers ↔ real numbers (8 ↔ 64).

- `GAP (PARTIAL)` **Divisibility only works for things that come in whole units.** Their counter-example: ages in the ratio 1 : 2 do not mean the age is even, because ages (and money, weights) need not be whole numbers. We say "if you're counting people, x is a whole number" (summary, g035), but we never give the case where it does *not* apply. **ADD AS ONE-LINE TIP** in g035.
- `GAP (PARTIAL, minor)` **Other ratios from the same ratio:** part : total, and part : difference (5 : 9 → girls : difference = 9 : 4). We have part : whole and "the rest" (wp-034), and the difference inside g035/g036. **SKIP**: it is covered in practice.
- `CHECK` They say "boys : girls is 5 : 9, so the number of boys divides by 5" without adding *only if the ratio is fully reduced* (6 : 8 does not mean a multiple of 6). Ours says it correctly (g035: "first reduce a ratio of people…"). Keep our wording.

## 5. Ratios for the Psychometric Entrance Test 2 [VayuqJpXWbE]
**Teaches:** a ratio after a change. Without the new ratio the result cannot be determined (2 : 9, eat 2 → it depends on the real amounts). With the new ratio, equalize the unchanged part, then the change in units = the real change. They also solve it with an equation.

- `GAP (PARTIAL)` **Adding or subtracting the same amount changes a ratio; multiplying or dividing both does not.** Their cookie example shows that after a fixed change the ratio cannot be determined. A real exam asks exactly this: **2022_winter_q2_09** ("In which case will the ratio between their salaries change?", where the answer is adding 3,000 to each). We have the multiply side ("we multiply both numbers, so the ratio stays", q-r26-t22-01) and the ages version ("the gap stays, only the ratio changes", g039). We never state the general rule. **ADD**: one sentence in wp-034, plus that exam-style question as a practice item (new numbers).
- The rest is covered: q-r26-t22-03 ("find the part nobody touched") teaches the same method in both ways.

## 6. יחסים לפסיכומטרי 3 [EuLylbRaOAw]
**Teaches:** worked ratio questions: a change (spiders/snakes), a **ratio of totals = product of two ratios**, a chain ratio through a shared member, whole : part → part : rest, a chain of "times" relations, a ratio equation with (a+b)(a−b), "if one number is tripled the ratio becomes 5 : 8", and a transfer plus people leaving.

- `GAP` **Product of two ratios.** Boys : girls = 3 : 4, and pimples per boy : pimples per girl = 2 : 7. So total pimples on boys : on girls = (3x·2y) : (4x·7y) = 6 : 28 = 3 : 14, using two different letters. Our stems have the ratio of two ratios (words-per-picture), a weighted fraction (bakery g045) and inverse rates (dishwashers g101). None of them has a count ratio × a per-item ratio. Exam: the same structure appears inside charts and percent questions (total = count × amount per item). **ADD** one practice question in t22 (new story and numbers). Its key line: "total = how many × how much each, so multiply the ratios."
- The rest is covered: chain ratio (q-r26-t22-01, plus the A:B, B:C stem), book : cover → book without cover (wp-034, Dan's bottle), transfers (g047, g050).

## 7. Rate Problems for the Psychometric Entrance Test 1 [svQZOhyn7M4]
**Teaches:** one worker. The ratio table and rate × time = work, what a rate means, unit conversions (min ↔ h ↔ s, 20 min = ⅓ h, 5⅓ h). A barber who **rests 10 minutes after every 30 minutes of work**. One worker doing two different tasks (tables then chairs).

- `GAP` **Work–rest cycles.** The barber works 30 min, rests 10, works 20 within one hour, so only 50 working minutes count, giving 120 braids. Nothing in t26 has cycles; the only break in our course is Ben's unpaid half hour (t22 stem). Related real items: **2019_winter_q1_20** (driver: 30-min trips, 10-min rest at each end, where is he at 10:00?) and **2025_autumn_q2_11** (print-shop rates "not including the breaks"). **ADD** one practice question in t26 (or t21 patterns). The idea: write out one cycle, count the working minutes inside the window, and remember that the last cycle may be cut short.
- `BETTER (small)` **Multiply or divide when converting units?** We give the rule ("from hours to minutes, we multiply by sixty", wp-092-2). They add a sense check: hours → seconds must give a *big* number, so multiply; dividing 5 by 3,600 would give almost nothing. Change: one line in wp-092-2: "Going to a smaller unit, you get more of them, so multiply."
- The rest is covered: two tasks one after the other (tailor stem), and units (wp-092-2).

## 8. Rate Problems for the Psychometric Entrance Test 2 [dQENwRfdI5k]
**Teaches:** several workers. Add rates, subtract for one who undoes the work, equalize times (common-time rows), the team formula W₁/(n₁t₁) = W₂/(n₂t₂) or a team table, and a worker who joins at 10:00 (with a clock-time answer).

- Nothing new. We cover: adding and subtracting rates (wp-094), stretching to a common time (wp-094-2), the team table plus the V method (wp-097/-2, which is the teacher's own method and clearer than their formula), joining in the middle (wp-099, q-r26-t26-03), and clock-time answers (g095).

## 9. בעיות הספק לפסיכומטרי 3 [FPQAp79knWY]
**Teaches:** worked work questions. Two diggers, barrels per hour asked in minutes, two rabbits where one stops halfway, rates with letters, a rate that triples, the **"B joins and the time halves → B is as fast as A"** idea, team (milkers), and a doubled rate.

- `GAP (PARTIAL)` **The reverse of "two equal workers take half the time".** If adding B halves A's time, B alone needs the same time as A. Our summary only gives the forward direction ("two equal workers? Half the time"). **ADD AS ONE-LINE TIP** in wp-094-2.
- The rest is covered: letters (g093), a rate that changes (q-r26-t26-01, compare by factors), a worker leaving (stems), team (wp-097).

## 10. בעיות חפיפה עם 3 קבוצות [cFzm56exsJk]
**Teaches:** three overlapping groups. (a) Exact counts: 3 shows with 7 dancers each, 2 in all three, 5 in exactly two; how many danced in exactly one? They draw three circles, put the centre first, spread the "exactly two" over the pair regions, and fill each circle to 7, giving 5. (b) Range: each of 3 friends has 30 friends and every two share 10. How many are friends of all three? Answer 0–10. This is real exam **2023_autumn_q2_20**.

- `GAP` **Exact three-group counting ("exactly one / exactly two / all three").** We teach three groups only for at least / at most (r26-t24-three-groups, q-r26-t24-03). A clean rule for the exact case is: *sum of the group sizes = (exactly one) + 2·(exactly two) + 3·(all three)*, because each person is counted once for every group they are in. In the example, 21 = a + 10 + 6, so a = 5. We already have the two-group version ("exactly one: take both away twice", summary). Exam: this type does not appear in our 19 English sections. Their video says it has appeared recently, and the style guide lists a 3-circle Venn as "not in the Hebrew" (new material). **ADD AS ONE-LINE TIP plus one guided question**, if you approve new material. Otherwise SKIP.
- (b) is fully covered: our lesson gives "the most in all three is the smallest pair" and "zero is possible", on exactly this structure.
- `CHECK` Their way of placing the 5 "exactly two" dancers arbitrarily among the pair regions works only because the question asks for a total that does not depend on how they are split. A student could take away "pair regions can be filled any way you like". If we add this type, teach the counting formula above instead.

## 12. Psychometric Exam- Motion Problems 2 [bpTK6SwYLTE]
**Teaches:** two bodies. Toward each other → add the speeds; same direction → subtract. A chase in three steps (head-start gap → closing speed → time) with the check *"time since the chaser left, or since the first one left?"*. Circular track: opposite directions meet after one lap together, and "5 laps more" = 5 × the lap length in metres. A meeting after a head start (pig and lion).

- `GAP (PARTIAL)` **From whose start is the time counted?** They end the chase by checking whether the question asks for the time since the chaser left (closing time) or since the first runner left (closing time + head start). Real exam **2026_spring_q2_17** counts the catch-up "after 30 seconds from the beginning of the game", which includes Hodaya's 20-second head start, so Rachel runs only 10 s and the answer is ×3. **2020_autumn_q2_18** (tortoise and hare, delay T) has the same timeline issue. Our wp-113 / g116 teach the head-start gap but never this reading check. **ADD AS ONE-LINE TIP** in wp-113 and the t27 summary. This matters: it is a real exam trap.
- The rest is covered: relative speed (wp-113), the head start then together (g115), circular laps (g121, q-r26-t27-03, summary "a lap gained").

## 13. Psychometric Motion Problems 3 [KFwjexMFvVM]
**Teaches:** worked motion questions. Weekly running minutes at "100 m per half minute", swimming against a current, a square perimeter vs its diagonal in equal time (speed ratio = distance ratio), a goblin and a dog chase, **two cars at the same speed leaving 2 hours apart**, distance in metres with letters, a speed that **halves every half hour**, and average speed over two equal laps (pick a friendly distance).

- `GAP (PARTIAL)` **Same speed, staggered start: the gap never changes.** It equals speed × delay (2 hours apart, 220 km apart → v = 110). We have nothing on this in t27. Exam: rare (motion has 12 questions in 19 sections). **ADD AS ONE-LINE TIP** in wp-113 ("same direction, same speed: the gap stays as it was"). It also fits the "gap stays, ratio changes" idea from ages.
- `GAP (PARTIAL)` **A speed that keeps halving: give the letter to the last, smallest speed** (v, 2v, 4v) so there are no fractions. This is our "Ben is the smaller one" idea (wp-034) applied to motion, but we never apply it outside ratios. **ADD AS ONE-LINE TIP** (t27 rewrite, or the wp-037 tip: "when amounts keep halving, call the smallest one x").
- `CHECK` **Goblin and dog:** the transcript says the dog catches the goblin "8 seconds" after it starts, but they solve 80/(v − 120) = 8 with speed in m/min, which gives v = 130 m/min. With 8 seconds (= 2/15 min) the answer would be 720 m/min. Either the original stem says 8 minutes (and the auto-transcript is wrong), or their solution forgets the unit conversion they had just stressed. Don't copy this item without checking it.
- The rest is covered: current (q-r26-t27-02, summary), equal time → speeds follow distances (wp-110), a friendly distance for average speed (wp-108-after), letters (q-r26-t27-06).

## 14. קומבינטוריקה (צירופים) 2 [IiRmQb_27kU]
**Teaches:** arrangements. n! in a row, (n−1)! in a circle (the first seat is only a reference point), gluing a pair, **two separate glued blocks** (3 mice together and 4 presidents together → 2!·3!·4! = 288), identical items (divide by k!), and two kinds of repeats (5!/(3!·2!) = 10).

- `BETTER` **Why we divide by k! for identical items.** Ours (q-r26-t28-08, BANANA): "Swapping the As changes nothing. Divide by three factorial." They draw all 24 orders of 4 coloured circles, then paint 3 of them the same colour, and the 24 collapse to 4: each pattern appears 3! = 6 times. For a weak student, *seeing* the repeats is far more convincing than being told. Change (in the t28 rewrite): open the repeats rule with a tiny example that can be listed in full. For instance, A A A B: 4! = 24 if all were different, but only 4 rows really exist (BAAA, ABAA, AABA, AAAB), because each one was counted 3! = 6 times. Then go to BANANA.
- `GAP (PARTIAL)` **More than one glued block.** Each block is arranged inside itself (its own factorial), and the blocks plus the other items are arranged as units. Ours glues only one block (q-r26-t28-06, q-09, stems). Exam: glue questions are rare in our sections. **ADD AS ONE-LINE TIP** in r26-t28-arrange.
- The rest is covered: n! (g130), round table (q-r26-t28-09 plus a stem), glue (q-06), repeats (q-08).

## Top recommendations (most valuable first)

1. **Ratio change rule (t22, wp-034, plus one practice item).** Multiplying or dividing both amounts keeps a ratio; adding or subtracting the same amount changes it (and alone, the result cannot be determined). A real exam asks exactly this (2022_winter_q2_09). We only have the ages version.
2. **Chase timing check (t27, wp-113 plus the summary).** Ask "time from whose start?": from the first runner's start, add the head start. This is a real exam trap (2026_spring_q2_17; also 2020_autumn_q2_18).
3. **Work–rest cycles (t26, new practice question).** Write out one cycle, count only the working minutes in the window, and watch for the last partial cycle. Related items: 2019_winter_q1_20 and 2025_autumn_q2_11.
4. **Wider trigger for the ratio table (t22 wp-028; show once in t23).** "Two numbers that describe the same thing (a percent and its amount, a fraction and a count) go in one row." Include the "what's left ↔ amount left" pairing (4/5 thrown, 32 left → 1/5 ↔ 32).
5. **Product of two ratios (t22, one practice question).** Total = how many × how much each, so multiply the ratios, using two different letters (3 : 4 and 2 : 7 → 3 : 14).
6. **Choosing the letter (t22 wp-037 tip).** The one after "than" gets the letter, written with its initial, so stage 3 shows at a glance which unknown you found. When amounts keep halving or thirding, call the smallest one x (this also applies to motion).
7. **Identical items shown, not told (t28 rewrite).** List a tiny case (A A A B → 4 rows, from 24 / 3!) before BANANA.
8. **Three groups, exact counts (t24, if you approve new material).** Sum of the groups = exactly one + 2·exactly two + 3·all three. One guided question.
9. **Small one-line tips:** "B joins and the time halves → B is as fast as A" (wp-094-2). "Going to a smaller unit → multiply" (wp-092-2). "Same speed, staggered start → the gap never changes" (wp-113). "Each glued block gets its own factorial" (r26-t28-arrange). "Divisibility from a ratio only for whole things, not ages or money" (g035).
10. **Don't copy:** their goblin-and-dog chase (the units don't match the stated 8 seconds), and their "spread the pair regions any way" fill for three groups. Keep our "reduce the ratio first" wording for divisibility.
