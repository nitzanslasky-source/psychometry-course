### The additions

| Video | Concept | What was added | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| solve-geo32-g044 (¶2.5) | Stop at s²: the area is the side squared | "This comes back a lot. When they ask for the area of a square, and you reach s squared, stop there. You don't need the side itself, so there's no root to take. Here s would be root ninety-eight, and we never needed it." | Codex marked this as the NEW idea of #214. It was one line before. It is the reusable time-saver for every square or "area from a relation" question, and it stops students taking needless roots. |
| solve-geo32-g048 (¶2.6) | Look for the combination, not each letter | "This idea comes back a lot. When we can't find each letter on its own, we look for the combination the question needs. Here x needed three alpha plus three beta, and that's three times the sum we knew. So we never needed alpha or beta alone." Board: "Need the combination, not each letter". | This is the NEW strategy of #218, and it recurs in angle questions with letters (g071) and in algebra. Before, it was only "We never found alpha or beta". |
| solve-geo32-g058 (¶1.9) | A bisector in a parallelogram cuts off an isosceles triangle: the why | "Why does it always happen? The bisector cuts the angle into two equal parts. The opposite sides are parallel, so the Z gives that same angle again at the other end. So the triangle has two equal angles, and it's isosceles." Board: "bisector + Z → isosceles". | The script gave the pattern as a slogan to remember. Now it comes from the bisector and the Z, both already taught, so a student can rebuild it for any parallelogram or trapezoid with a bisector. |
