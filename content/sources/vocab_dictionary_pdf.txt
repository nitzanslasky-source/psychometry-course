Study the relationship, not just the definition
For an analogy, begin with the word that is easiest to explain through its partner. For time : clock, start
with clock: a clock is a tool that measures time. Then preserve the printed order when checking another
pair.
Read the meaning, cover it, and explain the word in your own words. Make up a new example. For a
word with several meanings, ask which meaning the question needs. Revisit words you cannot yet
explain comfortably.
How to read this dictionary
EXAM ANALOGY marks vocabulary found in analogy stems or choices. EXAM VOCABULARY marks other
words found in the verbal sections. ADDITIONAL WORD marks a useful study suggestion that was not
found by the word-form check. These are not predictions of future questions.
Sources refer to the PDF page number, which may differ from the number printed on the exam page.
Attestation can be the headword or a related form, such as hesitant and hesitantly. Definitions and
examples here are newly written.
The selection focuses on useful general vocabulary from medium level upward. Proper names, specialist
passage-only labels, and ordinary beginner words have been left out.
