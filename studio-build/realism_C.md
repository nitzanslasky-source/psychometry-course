## Reading comprehension

**Format note.** The real Hebrew Winter 2025 sections (23 questions) have **two short passages** (A: Q18–20, B: Q21–23). Each is about 15–16 Hebrew lines, 3 paragraphs and 3 questions. Winter 2024 §1 still has **one long passage** (about 32 lines, 4 paragraphs, 6 questions, Q18–23).

NITE's official English translations always have one passage with 6 questions (Q15–20), 40–45 lines, about 640–710 words. I measured this on clean passages from Spring 2019, Summer 2020 and Summer 2022. No official English translation of the Winter 2025 or Winter 2024 passages exists in exams_new_mix.pdf, so I compared against the Hebrew.

**All 16 course passages are 600–676 words, with 4–6 paragraphs and 6 questions.** That fits the English-translation format well. For Winter 2025, the course expanded each 3-question Hebrew passage to about 2.3 times its length and doubled its questions.

## Overall

**Summary of ratings.** Topic 47: 5, 5, 5, **2**, 3. Topic 48: 3, 5, 5, 5, 5, 5, 4. Passages: 3, 3, 2 (for originality).

**What the course items are.** All 12 inference items are **translations of real NITE items** (8 from Hebrew). Two of them, 0214 and 0217, are near-verbatim copies of NITE's official English. That is why most of them read exactly like the real exam. Length, option style and difficulty match by construction. The problems are in the places where the translator *changed* something.

**Typical differences:**
1. **Edits that broke the logic.** One edit created a second correct answer (0226, option 3). One removed the premise the key depends on (0217). One dropped a clause and left an unused name behind (0263). One lost the language-specific trap in translation (0239). Every weak item is a place where the translator changed something. None is a faithful translation.
2. **Wording tics.** Negative stems in capitals ("CANNOT", "NOT", "FIRST"); official English writes them in lowercase. The generic "he/his"; official English since 2020 uses singular "they". "According to the passage" where official English says "the paragraph" for short inference texts. Otherwise the register is right: formal, precise and slightly dense.
3. **Passages are reskins.** Each passage transposes the real passage's argument sentence by sentence into a new domain and clones the question sets (3 of 6, 3 of 6, and 6 of 6 respectively), including the option templates and key positions. Length (600–650 words), paragraphing, vocabulary level and question types all match NITE's English format well.
4. **The domain swap introduces factual errors that the originals do not have.** Invented statistics and a conclusion that goes against the evidence (Passage 1). False anchor definitions, the sonnet and the haiku (Passage 2). A claim misattributed to a real scientist (Passage 3). There is also a made-up proverb.
5. **Question order.** Passage 1 asks about paragraph 4 before paragraphs 2–3. NITE follows the order of the text.

**Recommendations:**
1. Apply the four required item fixes now: 0226 option 3 and the stem; the 0217 stem premise; 0263 "against Beni"; 0239 short words saved by condition B.
2. Do a house-style pass on all inference items: lowercase negatives, singular "they", "paragraph" for short texts, "Given:" or "It is known that:".
3. Passages: keep the lengths and question types, but break the one-to-one mapping. Use a different order of moves, rewrite at least half the questions with new logic, and shift the keys. Passage 3 needs this most.
4. Fact-check each reskinned domain before publishing. Use factual claims only, invent a scholar rather than misquote a real one, and use airtight anchor examples.
5. Fix the passage line references (Passage 3's Q3 and Q5), and check all "(line N)" references against the rendered line numbering.
6. Policy note for Nitzan: the verbal notes in work/verbal (NOTES.md) say real questions stay internal and generated content must be original. These 12 video items are translations of real questions (two near-verbatim from NITE's official English), and the passages are close structural reskins. Whether that is acceptable for the course is your decision.
