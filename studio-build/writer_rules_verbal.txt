THE TEACHER'S RULE FOR VERBAL (most important)
- Verbal reasoning is about reading fast and drawing exactly the right conclusion; a translated question changes its
  difficulty. So: from the Hebrew subtitles take ONLY the concepts, the lesson structure and order, the methods,
  the tips/warnings and the closing verdicts. NEVER translate or adapt a Hebrew example question or Hebrew sentence.
- Every example and every guided question comes from the English question source named in your task
  (bank file vcands<topic>.txt; for Analogies/Active reading the course's own questions in tv<topic>.txt).
- Never change a single word of a question stem or answer choice. Quote them exactly when you read them aloud.
- Map each Hebrew worked example to ONE English question of the SAME TYPE and a similar difficulty (the Hebrew says
  "רמת קושי קלה/בינונית/גבוהה" — easy / medium / hard; say the level on the title slide script like the Geometry modules).
  Teach the Hebrew method(s) for that example, in the Hebrew order, on the English question.
- Prefer bank items marked [NITE key] or [real exam], then [keyed]. Do NOT use [VERIFY] or [inf_v1 REVIEW] items as
  guided questions. Solve every chosen question yourself first; if you disagree with the bank's answer, don't use it
  (list it in your report).
- If a Hebrew method genuinely doesn't apply to any available English question, teach the concept on a concept slide
  (short pop-ins, no invented question) and say in your report which method had no matching question.
- If the Hebrew relies on something Hebrew-specific (a Hebrew word pair, Hebrew grammar, Hebrew connector words), teach
  the English equivalent concept instead (English connectors, English word relations) — and say so in your report.

STRUCTURE
- Follow the Hebrew lesson structure for your range: one lesson() per Hebrew lesson segment (theory), then the guided
  questions that the Hebrew solves after it, grouped with guided() (one group per lesson; group title e.g.
  'Sentence Completion Questions I'). Module ids: lessons 'vr<topic>-<short-slug>'; guided ids are automatic
  ('solve-'+qid). The build numbers modules and renames guided sidebars to "Question N" per topic.
- Lesson sidebar: <= 13 items, each <= 22 characters. Title slide first (clean: only spoken lines).
- Concept slides start blank; each rule/term/formula pops in with A(label, T(...)) — full width (x=410, w=1140),
  y values increasing (or omit y to flow down). Keep pop-ins short (one line or two).
- You may put a real English example on a lesson slide as mode='question' with pre=[VQ(qid)] (bank/course question),
  e.g. to demonstrate a method during the lesson. Then that qid must not be reused elsewhere.
- Guided question slides: the full question is pre-loaded (VQ, automatic in guided()). The stem sits at the top and the
  four answers are stacked rows at the bottom; the space between is for pop-ins. Put method pop-ins with
  P('short text', y=...) — they auto-stack under the question text. Max 2–3 short pop-ins per slide; if you need more,
  make another slide.
- D('...') = the teacher's hand annotations ON the text: underline a key word, circle a connector, bracket a clause,
  write a short arrow/label, cross out an answer. The last solving slide must contain D('Circle choice N') with the
  correct N, followed by the spoken line "Choice N." (N = the correct answer, 1-based).
- Reading comprehension: show passage paragraphs with a question-mode slide whose pre is [PSG(pid, [n, …])]
  (pass it via guided slide tuple extra: ('Read paragraph 2', [...], dict(pre=[PSG('vbp-03',[2])])) ), then the question slide.
