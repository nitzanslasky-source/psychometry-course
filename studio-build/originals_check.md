## Problems and fixes

### 1. vo-45-001: second defensible answer (option 3)
The paragraph contrasts artificial organs, which are judged against a living model, with inventions named after the earlier thing they replaced ("wireless telegraph"). Option 3, "Calling a film 'a play without a stage'", fits the same pattern: film (cinema) is an invention, and it is named after the earlier medium it succeeded, minus its defining element. The explanation's defense ("names a work of art, not an earlier device") is thin, because nothing in the stem limits the pattern to devices.
**Fix:** replace option 3 with a name that does not refer to a predecessor. For example, "Calling a skyscraper 'a building that scrapes the sky'" (a name based on its appearance), or "Calling a submarine 'an iron fish'" (compared with a living creature, a second version of the option-2 trap). Update the explanation to match.

### 2. vo-44-003: originality, template clone of Summer 2020 §2 Q12
- The paragraph has the same skeleton: "[N]-year-old find ... attracted wide attention; two researchers, [X] and [Y], argue that the [artifacts] show that [society type A] had developed [organization] – a form of organization usually linked only with societies of a different type, [type B], which [arrived] [M] years later."
- "usually linked only with societies of a different type" is a near-paraphrase of the original's distinctive "generally associated only with societies of a different kind".
- All four options map one-to-one, and the key is almost verbatim: "According to Dalvik and Arkell's findings, herding societies were already present ... 9,000 years ago" vs "According to Grosman and Munro's findings, farmer-producer societies already existed ... 12,000 years ago".

**Fix:** keep the mechanism (a "not implied" item whose key flips a timeline) but change the frame:
- drop the "two named researchers" device (for example, use a museum survey or a single report);
- rephrase the "linked only with societies of a different type" clause entirely (for example, "a practice that until now had been documented only among the herders who settled the island much later");
- replace at least two options with implications that do not map onto the original (for example, one about how the tools were dated, or about how the bog preserved them).

### 3. vo-47-005: originality, question sentence and distractors cloned from Summer 2020 §1 Q13
- The question sentence is nearly verbatim: "According to the paragraph, how is it possible to explain the fact that in areas where [X] is widespread, [resistant forms] appear more quickly than in other areas?"
- Each option is a direct transposition of the original's options:
  - "smaller percentage of flies are carriers" became "smaller proportion of the beetles carry the virus";
  - "greater number of tsetse flies" became "beetles are more numerous";
  - "genetic information transferred from the fly's body" became "exchange genetic material while on the mouthparts";
  - the key is the same.
- The causal chain is also identical: a vector picks up the pathogen from several hosts, the pathogen exchanges genes, and resistance follows.
- The mirrors field itself says "(English translation; solver's key)".

**Fix:**
- Reword the question, for example: "Which of the following, if true, best accounts for the finding that resistance-breaking forms of the virus arise sooner where the virus is widespread?"
- Replace two distractors with ones that are not in the original, for example: "In these areas, farmers plant older cucumber varieties that were never bred for resistance" (an outside-the-paragraph explanation), and "In these areas, each beetle feeds on only one plant during its life" (this contradicts the mechanism).
- Consider moving the key off position 1 or 4 so it differs from the original's position.
