NITE ENGLISH VOICE GUIDE (consolidated from blind "spot the fake" tests, rounds 1-2; the old running notes are in
blind_tells_log.txt, but THIS file wins where they differ). An expert who knows the English psychometric exam must
not be able to pick our items out of a mix with real ones. Every point below is something that got our items caught.
Keep each item's question TYPE, KIND of trap, answer position and rough difficulty; everything else (subject, names,
wording, option construction) may change. After editing, re-solve every item cold: exactly ONE defensible answer,
decidable from the item itself without outside knowledge.
Run: python3 /Users/nitzanslasky/psychometry-course/studio-build/lint_originals.py <GROUP>  (must pass).

A. VOICE
 1. Real NITE English is a professional translation from Hebrew: formal, plain, literal, slightly stiff, a little
    wordy; translation traces such as "if X - Y" conditionals, stems ending "... -", "is of the opinion that", "has
    no choice but to", comma-splice "however". Ours read as smooth native magazine, Wikipedia or essay English -
    the most common single tell. (Do not insert spelling or grammar errors.)
 2. No idioms/colloquialisms ("would do well", "trial run", "of modest means", "That said", "passed over"), no
    literary images, no clever turns ("lies not in X but in Y"), no punchline endings, no explanatory asides.
 3. No contractions anywhere. Hyphen " - " only, never en/em dashes. American spelling. Few semicolons.
 4. Generic pronoun: avoid needing one; when needed, "he" or "they" both occur in real items (older/newer exams).

B. CONTENT
 5. NOT famous material. Famous people, textbook cases and encyclopedia stories are a strong tell in EVERY type:
    Darwin, Mendel, Kafka, Galileo, Newton, Plato's cave, Fleming, Euler, Rosetta Stone, Napoleon, Aesop, Odysseus,
    Matthew effect, Simpson/Berkeley, chess memory, endowment/Hawthorne effects, Loftus, famous quotes (Pascal,
    Heraclitus, Churchill). Also no "In 1747 the Scottish naval surgeon James Lind ..." openings.
 6. What real items use instead: opinion/argument snippets on unusual subjects by little-known or invented writers
    ("Professor Shore", "historian Malkin", "the essayist Burak"); invented dialogues between two people; odd,
    specific invented entities (Zviel, Igleson, Mr. Mekam, the Dolmanian sculptor Ptolemy, Beznia, Upper Barbonia,
    Lundia and Sarvia, the Hothouse Theater, "vitamin Q", the Galilee Egg Company, AppleNet Company, Hatsavim School,
    the journal "Microscope"); odd concrete scenes (a compote, a king's chamomile, an orchestra's driver). Israeli
    context where natural (shekels, an Israeli town, a kibbutz, Hebrew names). Do not reuse these exact examples -
    invent your own in the same spirit.
    SAFEST pattern for paragraph/reading texts: an invented or unnamed writer arguing a view (opinions, not new
    factual claims). Background facts must be true and uncontroversial; never attach invented findings, quotes or
    opinions to real people or institutions.
 7. Not modern consumer/tech/Anglo-American life (apps, gyms, solar panels, cafés, marathons, quarterly reports,
    Oak Street, city council, community theater) and no news-style recent facts or dates after about 2020.
 8. Names: modern Israeli transliterations (Nechama, Chagit, Bentzi, Na'or, Gilad, Oded, Avner, Aya, Bella, Galit,
    Dorit, Galia, Erez, Gadi, Ella, Shira, Barak, Michal), sets of people often in Hebrew-alphabet order (Aya, Bella,
    Galit); researchers as "Professor X"/"Dr. X" with less common surnames. NOT the Bible set (Joseph, David, Ruth,
    Miriam, Shlomo, Esther), not stock surnames (Cohen, Levi, Katz, Mizrahi, Rosen, Shapira), not trendy names
    (Rotem, Lavi, Itai, Noa), not Anglo full names. Name things sparingly: no brand/band/album/street names.
 9. Sayings (parables): not stock English idioms (golden goose, cart before the horse, stable door) and not
    polished invented proverbs that fit the answer exactly; real ones sound translated and a little odd ("Every
    duck is a swan in its lover's eyes") or are plain concrete comparisons.
10. No English-specific language content (English word formation, spelling puzzles, letter codes, stress in
    English words); no textbook-term lectures (loanword, minimal pair, sentence types, symmetric relations).

C. SHAPE AND OPTIONS
11. NO CLONES: an expert who knows the mirrored real item must not be able to pair ours with it - do not keep its
    skeleton (stem formula + option layout + distractor order). Keep only the type and the kind of trap.
12. Key must not stand out: not the longest (lint: <= 30% of items), not the most detailed/hedged/qualified, not a
    near-verbatim lift of a sentence of the text, not the only option with a "since"/semicolon explanation. Long
    options are often WRONG in real items.
13. Distractors are plausible, locally coherent and as detailed as the key; they fail by subtle logic (reversed
    condition, necessary vs sufficient, "not necessarily", stacked negation, counterfactual, order of steps, one
    clause changed). Never crude antonym flips, nonsense, irrelevant trivia, self-contradicting options, straw-man
    absolutes, or mere reorderings of the key.
14. Real option sets are uneven and a little messy. Avoid: one phrase repeated verbatim in all 4 options,
    near-synonym distractor trios, graded-intensifier sets, symmetric 2x2 grids, perfectly parallel templates.
15. Stems use only formulas attested in the real sample (see your profile file); they do not explain their own
    logic, and options do not carry their own justification.
16. Real items are rarely one-dimensional: a single clean textbook flaw with an obvious answer looks fake;
    many real items need two steps.

D. TYPE-SPECIFIC
17. Completions: one or two dense sentences, 3-4 blanks, trap in the connectors; fills of uneven length;
    one-blank items test MEANING with same-part-of-speech words, never grammatical form. No analogy mapping,
    principle application, dialogue or litotes puzzles dressed as completions; no encyclopedic prose with one
    sentence-length blank; every wrong option survives blank 1 and fails only on the whole sentence.
18. Rules/arrangements: small everyday settings (salaries of three people, four cars, a square table, a school's
    bylaws), wordy but logically flat. No legal drafting ("- and only on such days -", "Notwithstanding"), defined
    terms in quotes, abstract symmetric tables (salts P/Q/R/S), knights-and-knaves, loophole-closing clauses.
19. Science: quirky invented settings, not famous studies or taught named designs (lottery randomization,
    knockout mouse).
20. Reading: stems point to places with a parenthetical reference exactly where NITE puts "(line N)" - ours
    use "(paragraph N)": "The word 'surprisingly' (paragraph 3) ...", "'this' (paragraph 2) refers to -". Mix stem
    formats as real sets do (slash-option blank fills, reference questions, "explains the use of the word",
    analogy items, long overlapping options); no graph questions; 590-720 words, 4-6 paragraphs; not built
    backwards for a structure question.

E. METHOD (added after round 3 - the voice tell persists when we write in English)
21. WRITE IN HEBREW FIRST, THEN TRANSLATE - exactly how NITE produces its English exam. For each item: (a) write the
    complete item in Hebrew as a NITE item writer would (Hebrew phrasing, Hebrew connectors like "לפיכך", "אף על
    פי כן", "קל וחומר", Israeli setting and names); (b) translate it into English as a faithful, literal,
    professional translation - keep Hebrew sentence order and connectors where English allows, do not smooth or
    polish, do not add clauses the Hebrew lacks. Store the Hebrew draft in an internal field "he_draft" (never shown
    to students). Compare the English with 3 real items of the same type before saving.
22. Do not over-engineer: no planted explanatory clause whose only job is to make the key airtight; connector
    options differ in LOGIC (therefore / nevertheless / however), never near-synonyms (in addition / on top of that);
    minimal-pair options (surprising / not surprising) at most in some items, not as the file's pattern; no neat 2x2
    grids of option halves; some distractors wrong in their own odd way.
23. Paragraph-completion options (a whole sentence completing a text) end with a period, as in real items.

F. ROUND 3 (completions)
24. Invented COUNTRY/PLACE names used as adjectives (Oshrian, Tolvian, Kardonian, "Upper X") are now a tell by
    overuse. Default to generic or real settings (Israel, Haifa, a town in the north, Western Europe, a certain
    country, Rome); use at most an occasional invented culture. Characters: bare surname (Eldad, Talmon), first name
    only (Nili, Yoav, Dina), or "Professor/Dr. X" - NOT "The [profession] [first name + surname]" and never invented
    full names just to vary options.
25. Every slot in every option must do logical work: no filler slot of near-synonyms ("fell by half / was cut in
    half", "twenty years / two decades"), no long clause copied verbatim across options, no punctuation-only or
    fixed connector slots. Real items change almost every slot.
26. Scenarios: fewer village/institution anecdotes with one causal twist; more abstract claims about a thinker's or
    researcher's view, nested negations/counterfactuals, sayings, rule-like statements.
27. STYLE DONOR METHOD (use for every item from now on - it prevents the whole file from sharing one set of habits,
    which is what testers keep catching): for each of our items, pick a DIFFERENT real item of the same type from
    your real sample (not the mirrored source; use each donor at most once) and match its SURFACE: number of
    sentences and their length, how people are named (or not), setting type, register and translation traces,
    option lengths and how many slots change, whether options end with periods. Take NO content, names or
    distinctive wording from it. Record the donor's source label in an internal field "style_donor".
    Combined with point 21 (Hebrew first), this is the method; the example lists in this guide are illustrations,
    not a palette to reuse.
28. CORRECTION: option LENGTHS must vary a lot, as in real items (a one-line option next to a two-sentence one).
    The old lint rule "no distractor under 45% of the key" made our sets evenly balanced, which is itself a tell -
    it is removed. Only the key-is-longest share (<= 30%) remains. Do not make all four options full compound
    clauses of the same shape.
29. Invented LOCAL place and institution names (the village of Tzurim, Kiryat Levona, Tavor Insurance, Emek
    College) and titled full names ("Dr. Menucha Kalir") are tells; real items say "a municipality", "a coastal
    city", "a pharmaceutical company", or use one plain first name / a surname. Never reuse a name across items.
    Invented rustic proverbs with farm images are a tell; prefer recognisable real sayings or plain comparisons.

G. ROUND 4 RESULT AND FINAL RECIPE
30. P41b PASSED round 4 (tester caught 0 of 33 and wrongly accused 16 real items). Its recipe: Hebrew draft first,
    then a PROFESSIONAL translation - not word-for-word. Every other group overshot into calques and was caught for
    them. Translation traces belong ONLY in connectors, set phrases and a slight formal stiffness ("It is not
    surprising that", "had no choice but to", "is of the opinion that", "all the more so", "to their bad luck",
    "a threshold requirement"). Grammar, word order and possessives must be normal edited English:
    "Rina's conclusion" (never "the conclusion of Rina"), "his son's books", "the auditor's report", "he also read
    the letters" (never "read also the letters"), no sentences opening "Also ...", no "for two years already", no
    "If so, although", no "suggested to her to", no "see in X the Y", no resumptive pronouns ("whoever ..., he").
    Read content/verbal_originals_P41b.json (items 135-167) before writing: that is the target voice.
31. Invented authorities with Hebrew surnames + job label ("Dr. Handelsman, who studies folk tales") in every item
    are a tell; vary: some items with no named source, some dialogues between two first-name characters, some
    unnamed "researchers", occasional invented countries. Do not tag every scenario with an Israeli place.
32. Paragraph and reading texts: real ones are denser and carry detail that does not affect the answer (figures,
    dates, extra facts). Reading passages may rest on real, well-documented subjects with real named researchers and
    figures, described accurately (list them in "facts"), as in round 1 - but in the recipe-30 voice and without a
    fixed "accepted view -> scholar objects -> critics" arc.
