# Topic 27: Motion

**Depends on:** work and rates (T26). The whole topic opens with "a motion problem is just a work problem … cracking seeds" (video 1, slides 1–2). Ratios, direct and inverse (T22: "Speed Ratios" video). Weighted averages (T25: Average Speed slide 4). Percent increase as a multiplier (T23: Q12). Equations with fractions (T6–T7: Q11, p17). Dividing by a fraction (T2–T3: Q8 "1 : ⅔", Q9). Scientific notation / exponent laws (T8, T10: p13). Pythagoras and the 5-12-13 triple (T31 Triangles: Q2). Central angle of an arc (T33 Circles: p10).
**Needed by:** nothing directly. Motion can sit anywhere after T26, but Q2 and p10 need geometry (T31, T33), which comes later. Either move those two, or add a one-line reminder in the question.

## 1. Coverage — what is missing

- **Train / object length.** "A train passes a post / a tunnel / another train." Classic exam type. It appears only in p25, with no lesson. Add one slide in "Relative Speed": distance = tunnel + train length. Also two trains passing each other: distance = sum of lengths, speed = relative speed.
- **Current and wind (upstream / downstream).** Only p24, and only downstream. Not taught. Add: downstream = boat + current, upstream = boat − current. The exam type is: "given both trip times, find the current". Half the difference of the two speeds = current.
- **Circular track, opposite directions.** p10 and p19 need "together they cover one lap per meeting". The video and Q13 teach only the same-direction case. The memory card also has only the same-direction row. Add a row: "Circular, opposite directions → add speeds; they meet every time their total = one lap."
- **Distance–time graphs.** Missing completely. The exam sometimes gives a graph: slope = speed, flat segment = stop, crossing lines = meeting. Add one short slide and 2 practice questions.
- **Answers in letters.** Only p02 and p03. The exam often asks "a car goes d km in t hours…" with expressions in the choices. Teach "plug in numbers for the letters" once (it is taught for factorials in T28, not here).
- **Percent change of speed → percent change of time.** Q12 touches it. Give the rule as a small table: speed +25% → time −20%; +50% → −33⅓%; +100% → −50%. Very common exam trap ("speed up by 20% — by what percent does time drop?").
- **Meeting twice / meeting then continuing** (two walkers meet, then continue to the ends and turn back). At the second meeting they have covered 3 lengths together. This is the exam-hard type. Missing.

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- Video 1, slide 3: "Beside the triangle…", "the triangle shows the rest". The board list shows no triangle. If it is not drawn, the weak student is lost. Draw the d / v·t triangle on the board.
- A **distance–speed–time table** is used in Q3 ("Logic, then a table") and Q11 ("A distance–time–speed table") but is never taught. Add one slide in video 1: rows = each person / each leg, columns = distance, speed, time.
- Minutes as fractions of an hour are used everywhere (20 min = ⅓, 40 = ⅔, 12 = ⅕, 45 = ¾). Put a small table on the memory card and on slide 4.
- Q11: "Multiply by thirty-six" — no word on why 36 (LCD of 12 and 18, or 24 and 18). Q9: "12 : 9/60 = 12 × 60/9" is fast. Say one line: "dividing by a fraction = multiplying by its reciprocal".
- The written solutions often use "so" in the middle of the sentence: "Their distances so have…" (Q4), "A so covers…" (Q5), "Their separation so grows…" (Q6), "The trip is so three seconds longer" (Q10), p22. This reads as an error. Use "So their distances have…" or "therefore".

**Medium student**
- The ratio rules (same time / same distance) are clear. But there is no drill that asks *which* quantity is fixed. Add a 4-question quick check: "What is fixed here?" before Q4.
- Relative speed: good. The head-start step (Q7, Q8) is well shown. The two-method check in Q8 is excellent.
- Average speed video says "Honestly? Rare on the exam." Then Q3 and p21 test it. Keep the warning short, or cut it; students skip what is called rare.

**Strong student**
- Q1, Q6, p07, p22, p23 are too easy and repeat. They will skim.
- Q12 method 3 stops too early ("it doesn't settle this one"). It can settle it: 1.5 times as fast → 6 hours. 1.8 is between 1.5 and 2, so the time is strictly between 4.5 and 6 → 5. Show this; it is the kind of estimate the strong student wants.
- Q13 method 1 says "Not easy — and not how we'll do it on the exam." Fine, but make method 2 first, then the equation as a check.
- Wants: the missing types above, plus 2ab/(a+b) for equal distances as an optional check (72, 48 → 57.6).

## 3. Correctness

- **All keys are correct.** Q1–Q13 and p01–p27. No wrong answers found. I solved all practice questions.
- Solution videos for Q10 and Q12 show "180−meter" and "1,350−kilometer" with a minus sign instead of a hyphen (same bug in T28: "three−digit", "two−way"). Display bug in the pre-loaded question text.
- p15: "first catch it" — at t = 0 both vans are at the start. Clear enough, but "first reach the stopped van" is cleaner.
- Memory card, Relative speed table: the "circular track" row covers only the same direction, but p10 and p19 need the opposite direction.

## 4. Methods and tricks to add

- **Estimate first, then calculate** (Q3 and Q4 do this well). Make it a named step in the recap: "Which side of the middle? Cross out."
- **Speed/time percent table** (see Coverage) on the memory card.
- **Equal times → simple average; equal distances → closer to the slower speed.** One line on the Average Speed recap. Q3's written solution says it; the video does not.
- **Sketch first** is said many times. Show one full sketch habit on the board: dots for start points, arrows, gap marked. The (does:) lines mostly write numbers, not the drawing.
- **Work back from the answers** (Q11 method 2) — also good for p16, p17, p20. Mention it in the recap.

## 5. Practice

- 27 practice questions + 13 guided. Enough in number.
- Order is not easy → hard. p19 (hard) is followed by p21–p23 (very easy). Reorder: basics first, then ratios, relative speed, circular, special types.
- Repetitive: p23 ≈ Q1 (m/s → km in 25 minutes, same numbers pattern); p21 ≈ Q3; p14 ≈ Q13; p07 and p22 are one-step.
- Practice uses methods **not taught**: p24 (current), p25 (train length), p10 and p19 (circular, opposite directions), p13 (scientific notation, not really motion).
- Missing exam-hard: meeting twice, graph, answers in letters beyond p02/p03, a "by what percent does the time change" question.

## 6. Verdict

- **Weak:** can do one-step d = v·t and simple meetings; will get lost on unit conversions with fractions of an hour and on Q11-type equations.
- **Medium:** solid on ratios, meetings and chases; will miss train-length, current and opposite-direction circular questions.
- **Strong:** will finish fast but meets exam-hard types (meeting twice, graphs, percent speed/time) unprepared.

**Top 3 changes**
1. Add a short "special cases" video: train length, upstream/downstream, circular track in both directions (with memory-card rows). These are already in practice (p10, p19, p24, p25) with no lesson.
2. Teach the distance–speed–time table and a minutes-to-hours fraction table in video 1, and actually draw the triangle.
3. Add the speed/time percent table and finish Q12's estimate; add 3–4 exam-hard practice questions (meeting twice, graph, letters) and cut p22/p23.

# Topic 28: Counting possibilities

**Depends on:** trial and error / organised listing (T21: slide 4 "just like many other question types"). Fraction cancelling (T2–T3: Q11, Q13). Exponent laws (T8: p16 "9^x = 3^{2x}"). Prime factorisation (T14: p02). Divisibility and digit sums (T15–T16: p20). Solving n(n−1) = 90 by consecutive numbers (T6–T7, T16: Q16, p12). Polygon and diagonal definitions (T34 Polygons, later: slide "Diagonals", Q8).
**Needed by:** T29 Probability (almost everything: ordered outcomes of two dice, complements, choosing a group). T34 Polygons (the diagonals formula is taught here).

## 1. Coverage — what is missing

- **Paths in a road diagram.** "From A to B there are 3 roads, from B to C there are 2 roads; how many routes from A to C? Through B or through D?" Multiply along a path, add between paths. Very typical NITE item with a drawing. Missing.
- **"Must be together / must not be together."** Only p23 (together), not taught. Add the "glue" trick: tie the pair into one item, then × 2 for the inner order. Not together = all − together.
- **Identical items** (LEVEL, p25) and **circular tables** (p26): in practice, not taught. Either teach one slide each (divide by repeats; fix one person on a round table) or remove them.
- **Zero in digit questions with no repetition.** Classic trap: even 3-digit numbers from 0–5 without repetition → split by "last digit 0" vs "last digit 2 or 4". This is the best real example of "adding cases". Q9's cocoa example is artificial.
- **"Most restricted position first."** Said only in Q15's written solution. Q14's video starts with the free tens digit instead. Make it one clear rule on the board and the card.
- **"At least one" = all − none.** Very important for T29. The "Subtract" slide gives the idea but no "at least one" example. Add: 3-digit codes with at least one repeated digit = 10³ − 10·9·8.
- **Objects into boxes (which is the base?).** "4 letters into 3 mailboxes" = 3⁴, not 4³. Common trap; missing.
- **Unlabelled groups.** Q11 method 2 says "then we'd halve" but there is no question that practises it. Add one (split 6 people into two groups of 3 with no names → 10).

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- The first three videos (list, multiply, repetition) are clear and slow. Good.
- Q11 cancelling: "Cancel 8 with 4 and 2, and 6 with 3" is too fast. Write it as a fraction on the board, cancel one step at a time.
- "Two-Way Connections" video: title says "Two-Way Connections", teacher says "Mutual action". Use one name everywhere (the cards say "Mutual action").
- Q17 video, method 1: "Worried you missed one? Six is fewer than we've already found." This sentence is confusing. Say: "We found 7. Choice 6 is too few. 42 and 720 would need many more rows, and there are no more."
- Q17 method 2: "choose the ONE who stays in place" — "in place" suggests he stands in the row. Say "stays out".
- Same "so" mid-sentence problem as T27: Q14 "That final position so has one choice", Q15 "A must so be odd", Q16 "The actual game count is so…".

**Medium student**
- There is no decision flow. The medium student needs one question order: (1) Does order matter? (2) Repetition allowed? (3) Any restriction → most restricted first; forced position = 1. (4) Double counting → divide by 2 or k!. (5) Cases or complement? Put this on the memory card as a 5-step checklist, before the table.
- "Add or Subtract Cases" says "very rare … one question every few years". "Choosing a Group" says "very hard, and very rare". Both ideas are needed in T29 and in p03, p11, p21, p22, p27. Calling them rare makes students skip them. Say "less common, but needed for probability".
- Q15 has three methods; good for this level.

**Strong student**
- Will be bored in Q2, Q4, Q5, Q6 and p04–p07 (all one-step).
- Wants: glue, gaps (p15 uses gaps without a lesson), identical items, round table, road diagrams, "at least one", and C(n,k) = C(n,n−k) in general (slide 4 hints at it: "eight of ten = two who stay out"; make it a rule).
- "Factorial Expressions" is good and fast. Keep it.

## 3. Correctness

- **All keys are correct.** Q1–Q17 and p01–p27. I solved all practice questions (p13 = 21, p15 = 120, p18 = 4, p19 = 36, p20 = 9 all check).
- p13 (keys and cupboards): the answer is right only because of "counting each successful opening as a test". The wording is very heavy and it is a worst-case puzzle, not counting possibilities. Simplify or move it to T21.
- Pre-loaded question text shows "three−digit", "two−way", "seven−sided" with a minus sign (display bug, same as T27).
- Q14 video says "I'll start with the tens digit — because nothing restricts it." It works here, but it contradicts Q15's rule "choose the most restricted position first". Start with the hundreds digit.

## 4. Methods and tricks to add

- **5-step checklist** on the memory card (see Medium student).
- **Glue and gap** slide in "Add or Subtract Cases": together → glue; not together → all − together; no two adjacent → place the others first, then fill the gaps.
- **Road-diagram slide** in "Multiply the Choices": "and" along a path = ×, "or" between paths = +. One drawing.
- **Check with a small case.** For any formula (n(n−1)/2, n(n−3)/2, ÷k!), test n = 4 by drawing. The strong student likes it; the weak student trusts the formula more.
- **Elimination by size.** Many choices differ by factors (Q5: 60 / 120 / 216; Q6: 120 / 360 / 720). Teach: "If order matters, the answer is the larger one; if not, divide."

## 5. Practice

- 27 practice + 17 guided. Enough.
- Spread: p01–p12 are easy and mostly one-step (p04, p05, p06, p07, p10 are the same skill). p13–p20 jump to puzzle level (p13, p15, p18, p20). There is little in the exam-medium middle.
- Not taught: p15 (gaps), p23 (glue), p25 (identical letters), p26 (round table). p02 and p16 test exponents more than counting.
- Missing: road diagram, zero-digit case split, "at least one", unlabelled groups, objects into boxes.

## 6. Verdict

- **Weak:** will handle stages, repetition, rows and handshakes; will get lost on dividing by k! and on case splits.
- **Medium:** reliable on the taught types; will lose points on together/not together, zero-digit cases and road diagrams.
- **Strong:** fast on everything taught; practice shows types (gaps, identical items, round table) that were never explained, so he must guess the method.

**Top 3 changes**
1. Add a 5-step decision checklist (order? repetition? restriction first? divide? cases/complement?) to the first card and use it in every solution video.
2. Add a short video (or extend "Add or Subtract Cases") with: zero-digit case split, "at least one" = all − none, glue for "together", road diagrams. Stop calling cases and groups "very rare" — T29 needs them.
3. Either teach or remove p15, p23, p25, p26; add 3–4 exam-medium practice questions in their place (road diagram, zero-digit even numbers, unlabelled groups, mailboxes).
