# Topic 30: Lines and angles

**Depends on:** simple linear equations (T6: p03 "9x = 180", p13, adv p15). Ratio "units" (T22: p17 "2:3:4"). Units digit idea (T16/T21: Q2 shortcut). Plugging in numbers (T20: Q3 method 2).
**Needed by:** every later geometry topic (T31 triangles, T32 quadrilaterals, T33 circles, T34 polygons, T37 coordinates). It must stay first in geometry.

## 1. Coverage: what is missing

- **"Don't assume" trap.** NITE figures are not drawn to scale. Lines are parallel or perpendicular only when it is given or marked. The lesson never says this. Add one line on the Recap slide: "Parallel only if given. 90° only if marked." Add one practice question where two lines look parallel but are not, and the answer is "cannot be determined".
- **Two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel.** Also: parallel to the same line → parallel. Adv p02 and adv p07 need these rules, but they are never taught. Add one sentence to slide 10.
- **Segments on a line.** Adv p03 (count the gaps, not the ticks), adv p09 and adv p16 (overlapping segments) are not taught anywhere. Either add a 1-minute slide ("AC + BD = AD + BC", "count intervals") or move these three questions to a topic that teaches them.
- **The zig-zag rule in general.** Q4 and p14 are the same shape (one bend, pointing out). Missing: (a) the bend pointing the other way (the "C" or "U" shape, where the angles add to 180 or x = 360 − a − b), and (b) two bends. The general rule is short: "angles that point left = angles that point right". The memory card tip hints at it. The video should teach it.
- **Co-interior angles (the "U"/"C").** The small + large = 180 idea covers it. But say it with a picture: "inside the U → sum 180".

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- Slides 2–9 are clear and slow. Good for this level.
- Slide 12 "Look for the Z": the teacher says "the two angles inside the corners of the Z are equal". But in figure v10 the two α marks are **outside** the Z (vertical to the Z corners). The weak student will not understand what "inside" means. Fix the figure (see Correctness).
- Slide 7 (vertical angles): figure v5 has one big arc that covers about 220°, starting at the 40° angle. The student cannot see which angle is 140. Fix the figure.
- Slide 4 says the exam's right-angle mark "almost always has a dot". None of the course figures has a dot (v2, g002, p02). Add the dot or drop the remark.
- Slide 6 defines "adjacent angles" as angles that add up to 180. Later (adv p08) two angles sit next to each other and do not add up to 180. Say "adjacent angles **on a straight line**".

**Medium student**
- The small/large method is excellent. It covers corresponding, alternate and co-interior angles with one idea. Keep it.
- Q3 (four lines, p, q, r, θ) is a big jump after Q2. Plugging in numbers saves it. Add one sentence: "choose values that make the four answers different". 150/150/150 works here, but students should know why.
- Adv p05 and adv p10 have no figure, and the wording is hard ("Its adjacent supplementary angle and the angle x together form an angle of 136°"). A medium student will draw it wrong. Add figures.

**Strong student**
- Slides 2–9 are too slow for this level. Add a "you can skip to slide 10" marker, or a 1-minute summary.
- The "flash of insight" in Q3 (p + q + r counts θ twice) is great. Give it a name: "overlapping angles: add them and subtract the full turn".
- Missing hard material: two bends, the U-shape, "not necessarily true" questions with figures that are drawn to mislead.

## 4. Methods and tricks to add

- **Units digit (Q2).** Great. Put it on the memory card with the example "83 + 54 + x = 180 → x ends in 3". The card has it only as a short tip.
- **Plug in for letters (Q3).** Add the rule: "choose values that look like the figure, and **different** from each other if the answers could tie."
- **Zig-zag rule** (after Q4): "Between two parallel lines, the sum of the angles pointing left = the sum pointing right." One picture with two bends. It solves p14, Q4 and the two-bend questions in one step.

- **Medium:** Mostly yes. Still at risk on unknowns and on untaught facts (perpendicular-to-same-line, segments).

# Topic 34: Polygons

**Depends on:** triangle angle sum and isosceles base angles, "the larger angle faces the longer side", the triangle inequality, and the exterior angle (T31: Q4, Q5, Q6, p06). Pythagorean triple 5-12-13 (T31: Q1). Silver 45-45-90 and golden 30-60-90 triangles (T31: Q3, Q6, Q7, Q9, p05, p16, p19). Equilateral triangle area a²√3/4 (T31: Q2, Q7, p24). Rhombus, square and trapezoid facts (T32: Q4 way 2, Q8, p01, p13). Inscribed angles, equal chords → equal arcs, radius ⊥ tangent, circle area (T33: slide 11, Q4 way 3, Q5, p09, p10, p12, p19, p20). Roots and estimating √2 ≈ 1.4, √3 ≈ 1.7 (T9–T10: Q6, Q8). Plugging in numbers (T20: Q8, Q9).
**Needed by:** T35 (prisms with hexagon/octagon bases), T36 (similar polygons), T38.
The current order (after T31–T33) is correct. Do not move T34 earlier.

## 1. Coverage: what is missing

- **Exterior angles.** The sum is 360° in every convex polygon, and each one is 360/n in a regular polygon. This is a core NITE fact, and it is never taught. But p21 and p22 use it in their solutions ("exterior turning angle"). Slide 10 is almost there ("central angle + one angle = 180"). Add: "The angle next to it outside (the exterior angle) = the central angle = 360/n." Then add the reverse question: **n = 360 / (180 − angle)**. Example: 150° → 360/30 = 12. That is the fastest method for "angle → number of sides" questions.
- **"Which could be an angle of a regular polygon?"** This is a classic type: 180 − angle must divide 360. Add one practice question.
- **Number of diagonals.** From one vertex: n − 3. In total: n(n − 3)/2. p23 needs n − 3, but it is never taught. Slide 4 (split into triangles) is the natural place: "from one vertex you draw n − 3 diagonals and get n − 2 triangles".
- **Polygons around a point / sharing a side.** For example, a square and a regular pentagon on a common side: what is the angle between them? (360 − 90 − 108 = 162). This is very common on NITE. Only p15 and p04 touch it, and neither is taught. Add one guided example.
- **Hexagon diagonal lengths.** Short diagonal = a√3, long diagonal = 2a. Q6, p07 and p19 all depend on this. Put it on the "Regular polygons — facts to know" card.
- **Concave polygons.** The folded hexagon (v487) is shown, but nobody says that the angle sum 180(n − 2) still holds, with one reflex angle. One sentence is enough.

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- The first video is clear. The "+1 side = +180°" slide and the "central angle, then complete to 180" tip are excellent for this level.
- Slide 10 says "Here's a table of the three regular polygons", but the board shows only the tip. The table is only on the memory card. Put the table on the board.
- Slide 11 proves "diagonals split the angle equally" with inscribed angles. This is fine only if T33 comes first. For a weak student, give the rule first, then the reason.
- "Haman's ear" (slide 4 of Two Hexagon Partitions): English-language students may not know the word. Add "(a three-corner cookie)", or call it the "star partition".
- Q9 and Q10 are hard. The weak student will need the octagon partition drawn many times. The practice has only 2 octagon partition questions (p17, p25).

**Medium student**
- Q4 (three ways), Q6 (math + estimate) and Q11/Q12 (formula + "+180 per side") are very good models.
- Q9 video: "In regular polygon questions the drawing is accurate… measure with your fingers." This is risky. The polygon is exact, but added points and lines may not be. Keep the real reason ("the square's side is a hypotenuse, AE is a leg"). Drop the finger measuring.
- p21 and p22 use a term that was never taught (see Coverage). A medium student will be stuck.

**Strong student**
- The octagon formula 2a² + 2a²√2 and the "triangles = square" insight are exactly what a strong student wants.
- Q5 slide 4 (hexagon is the balance point: side = r) is an excellent exam idea.
- Missing for this level: n = 360/(180 − angle), polygons meeting at a point, and a direct hexagon area (3√3/2)a².

## 3. Correctness

All 39 keys are correct (Q1–Q12, practice p01–p27). All the math in the videos checks out (Q1 pentagon angles are consistent: 90, 90, 90, ≈135, ≈135). Issues:

- **p12 solution** uses "the angle between a tangent and a chord is half the arc". T33 does not teach this rule (it teaches only radius ⊥ tangent). Use a taught route: OF ⊥ FT, triangle OAF is equilateral, so ∠OFA = 60° and ∠AFT = 30°. Then ∠ATF = 180 − 60 − 30 = 90°.
- **p21, p22** use "exterior turning angle" (not taught). **p23** uses n − 3 (not taught). The keys are correct.
- **Solution texts use ":" for division** ("5:√2", "25:4", "10:√3", "84:12"). English readers read ":" as a ratio. Use "/" or a fraction.
- **Typos (missing spaces):** p02 "side,4.8" and "side,2.4"; p10 "a135° sector"; p12 "hexagon’s120°"; p27 "triangle:48".
- **Figures:** all of them match their questions (checked: g106 is drawn to scale with CD = 17 and DH = 5; the p04 arrangement gives 5 outer sides per octagon; p09 α and β are in the right places; p12 T is placed correctly; p13 inner hexagon side = rhombus side; p15 α ≈ 144°; p16 is drawn to scale).
  - **p09** has an extra line FC that the question does not use. It is harmless but distracting. Remove it or use it.
  - **Question figures are small:** the drawing uses only about 250 px of a 640 px canvas (g110, g114, p02, p12, p13…), so the labels are tiny on a phone. Crop them or scale them up.
  - **Q4 (g110)** marks the angle as x, but the question asks for "angle ADB". Write "x = ∠ADB" in the question, or drop the x.
  - **v487** has an empty figure description. v490, v510 and v511: the "O" label sits on a radius line.

## 4. Methods and tricks to add

- **n from an angle:** n = 360/(180 − angle). Put it on slide 10 and on the memory card.
- **Anchor + 180 (Q11)** is great. Also add the reverse: "1,980 − 1,080 = 900 = 5 × 180 → 8 + 5 = 13 sides" (p14).
- **Hexagon quick facts card:** side = r, short diagonal a√3, long diagonal 2a, area = 6 equilateral triangles, triangle ACE = ½ of the hexagon.

## 5. Practice

- 27 practice questions, all in one set with no foundation/advanced split. The order is mixed: p01 (extended hexagon, medium) comes before p03 (easy angle sum). Sort them easy → hard, or split them into two sections like T30.
- Repetitive: p03 is the same as Q12's final step (900° → heptagon). p14 is the same as Q11.
- Missing: easy "one angle of a regular n-gon" (decagon, 12-gon) as a warm-up; "which could be an angle of a regular polygon"; square + pentagon at a common side; one more octagon-partition question at exam-hard level.
- Good exam-hard items: p04, p10, p13, p15, p19. They use the taught partitions well.

## 6. Verdict

- **Weak:** Can solve angle-sum and basic regular-polygon angle questions. Will struggle with octagon and hexagon area questions and with p21–p23 (untaught terms).
- **Medium:** Mostly ready. Gaps: exterior angles, diagonal count, polygons sharing a side.
- **Strong:** Well served by the octagon and hexagon partitions and the balance-point idea. Needs n = 360/(180 − angle) and polygons meeting at a point to be complete.

**Top 3 changes**
1. Teach exterior angles (sum 360, each 360/n, n = 360/(180 − angle)) and the diagonal count (n − 3, n(n − 3)/2). The practice already uses both.
2. Add one guided example of regular polygons meeting at a point or on a common side, plus 2 practice questions.
3. Put the pentagon/hexagon/octagon table on the slide-10 board, and add hexagon diagonal lengths (a√3, 2a) to the memory card. Also fix the p12 solution (tangent-chord rule not taught) and the ":" division notation in the solutions.
