### Q 1 (q-386): x and y are integers that are not prime, such that \(x < 20 < y\). Between x and 20 there are exactly 2 prime numbers (not including x). Between 20 and y there are exactly 2 prime numbers (not including y). What is the smallest possible value of \(x \cdot  y\)?
  (1) \(280\)
  (2) \(330\)
  (3) \(390\)
  (4) \(420\)
  correct: 3
  solution: Primes near 20: …, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29, 31, …. Below 20: for exactly the two primes {17, 19} to lie strictly between x and 20, x must be above 13 (to exclude 13) and below 17 — so x ∈ {14, 15, 16}, all non-prime; the smallest is x = 14 . Above 20: for exactly {23, 29} to lie strictly between 20 and y, y must be above 29 and at most 31; y = 31 is prime (forbidden), so y = 30 . Smallest product: 14 · 30 = 420 .

### Q 4 (q-389): a, b, c, d are distinct prime numbers (each different from the others). Given: \(x = {a}^{2} \cdot  b \cdot  {c}^{4} \cdot  d\) and \(y = {a}^{3} \cdot  {c}^{2} \cdot  {d}^{2}\). What is the greatest common divisor of x and y?
  (1) \(a \cdot  c \cdot  d\)
  (2) \({a}^{2} \cdot  {c}^{2} \cdot  d\)
  (3) \({a}^{3} \cdot  b \cdot  {c}^{4} \cdot  {d}^{2}\)
  (4) \(a \cdot  b \cdot  c \cdot  d\)
  correct: 1
  solution: For each shared prime take the minimum exponent: a: min(2, 3) = 2 → a² . b: appears only in x — excluded. c: min(4, 2) = 2 → c² . d: min(1, 2) = 1 → d . GCD = a²·c²·d .

### Q 5 (q-390): A number is divisible without remainder by both 8 and 12. What is the greatest guaranteed divisor of the number?
  (1) \(24\)
  (2) \(12\)
  (3) \(48\)
  (4) \(96\)
  correct: 0
  solution: A number divisible by both 8 and 12 is a common multiple of them, so it is divisible by their least common multiple. Factor: 8 = 2³ and 12 = 2²·3 ; the LCM takes the maximum exponents: 2³·3 = 24 . So 24 always divides the number. Nothing larger is guaranteed — the number could simply BE 24, and 48 does not divide 24.

### Q 8 (q-400): What is the sum of all numbers obtainable by multiplying together two or more of the following numbers: 2, 3, and 7?
  (1) \(42\)

### Q None (q-407): Between 44 and x there are exactly 2 prime numbers (not including x). Which of the following cannot be x?

  (2) \(56\)
  (3) \(58\)
  (4) \(60\)

  solution: The primes above 44 are 47, 53, 59, …. For exactly two primes to lie strictly between 44 and x, the interval must contain {47, 53} but not 59 — so x can be anything from 54 up to 59. ### Q None (q-408): a, b, c are distinct positive integers, and \(x = a \cdot  {b}^{2} \cdot  {c}^{3}\). Which of the following values is impossible for x?
  (1) \(24\)
  (2) \(21\)
  (3) \(40\)
  (4) \(72\)
  correct: 1
  solution: The factors may include 1. 24 = 6·2²·1³ ✓ (a=6, b=2, c=1) . 40 = 5·2³·1² — arrange as a=5 , b=1 , c=2 : 5·1²·2³ = 40 ✓. 72 = 9·1²·2³ ✓ (a=9, b=1, c=2) . For 21 = 3·7 : if c ≥ 2 then c³ ≥ 8 must divide 21 — impossible (8 ∤ 21, 27 > 21) ; so c = 1 , and then a·b² = 21 with b ≠ 1 (b distinct from c) needs b² ∈ {4, 9} dividing 21 — neither works. So 21 is impossible.

### Q None (q-409): a is an odd one-digit prime, and b is a two-digit prime smaller than 30. Given: \(x = a \cdot  b\). What is the most precise range for x?

### Q None (q-410): p, q, r are distinct prime numbers. Given: \(M = {p}^{2} \cdot  {q}^{3} \cdot  r\) and \(N = {p}^{3} \cdot  q \cdot  {r}^{2}\). What is the greatest common divisor of M and N?

### Q None (q-412): p and q are distinct prime numbers. Which of the following cannot be their difference?

### Q None (q-413): A number is called "interesting" if the sum of all prime numbers from 2 (inclusive) up to that number (inclusive) is itself prime. Which of the following numbers is an "interesting" number?

### Q None (q-414): a and b are integers. Given: \(a \cdot  b = 75\). Then \(a - b\) cannot be equal to:

### Q None (q-415): p and q are prime numbers with \(1 < p < q\), and \(p + q\) is also prime. Which of the following is necessarily true?
  (1) \(p + q = 13\)
  (2) \(p = 2\)
  (3) \(p + 7 < q\)
  (4) No such prime numbers exist
  correct: 1
  solution: If both p and q were odd primes, p + q would be even and greater than 2 — so composite, not prime. So one of them must be the even prime 2, and since p < q , it's p. ### Q None (q-416): Given: \(t > 1\) and \({t}^{2}\) is a prime number. Then t is necessarily —

### Q None (q-417): n is a positive integer. The number of distinct positive divisors of n (including 1 and n) is odd. Which of the following statements is necessarily true?
  (1) n is prime
  (2) n is \(\text{odd}\)
  (3) \(\sqrt{n}\) is an integer
  (4) \(\sqrt{n}\) is \(\text{odd}\)
  correct: 2
  solution: Divisors pair up as (d, n:d) , giving an even count — except when some divisor pairs with itself, i.e., d = n:d , which means d² = n .
