  [on screen] Blank

  [board] NOT diameter $\leftrightarrow$ chord — not the same kind of line
  [board] Linear ratio $=$ the ratio between two matching lines

  SAYS: When I have two similar shapes, I have matching lines between them.
  SAYS: Matching lines — the same kind of line.
  SAYS: Take two circles — they really are similar: a small circle and a big circle, the same shape.
  SAYS: If I compare a radius, what matches it in the other circle is the radius. Circumference with circumference.
  SAYS: What I can't do: take a diameter in one circle and a chord in the other. Not the same kind of line.
  SAYS: The linear ratio is the ratio between two matching lines.

  --- slide 6: Not only sides

  SAYS: Notice: the linear ratio isn't only between sides.
  SAYS: Not only the sides of the triangles — any pair of matching lines.
  SAYS: Heights, medians, diagonals — even differences between sides. Side minus side — that matches too.
  SAYS: For example, two squares — squares are similar: a small one, a big one, the same shape.
  SAYS: If the big square's diagonal is twice the small one's — then its side is twice as long too.
  SAYS: And differences: sides 8 and 5, difference 3. Double the shape — 16 and 10, difference 6. Doubled too.
  SAYS: One ratio between one pair of matching lines — it holds for all of them.
  --- slide 7: Area ratio
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] Area ratio $=$ (linear ratio)$^2$
  SAYS: Now — the area ratio.
  SAYS: In similar shapes, the ratio of the areas equals the linear ratio SQUARED.
  SAYS: If the linear ratio is 1 to 2 or 1 to 3 — the area ratio is that, squared.
  SAYS: Let's see why — or at least, let's see it.

  (does: Count the 9 small squares)
  SAYS: 1, 2, 3 … 9. Nine small squares.
  SAYS: So the linear ratio 1 to 3 — squared: 1 squared stays 1, 3 squared is 9. Area ratio 1 to 9.
  SAYS: Nine times the area — not three times.
  --- slide 9: Triangles too
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] [FIGURE: fig/v629.png - Similar right triangles with both legs enlarged by a factor of four]

  SAYS: A right triangle with legs 1 and 2. A bigger one: legs 4 and 8.

  --- slide 10: Square both parts
  [on screen] Blank

  [board] Area $25:49$ $\rightarrow$ linear $5:7$

  SAYS: And backwards: areas 25 to 49 — take the root of both — the linear ratio is 5 to 7.
  SAYS: Have the linear ratio? Square it — you've got the area ratio.

  --- slide 13: Recap
  [on screen] Blank

  SAYS: The linear ratio: between matching lines of the two shapes — always the same one.

### Q 2 (geo36-g137): AB is a diameter of a circle with radius 7 cm. Four congruent smaller circles have their centers on AB. Their diameters partition AB without gaps, as shown in the accompanying figure. What is the sum of the circumferences of the four smaller circles (in cm)?
  [FIGURE: fig/geo36-g137.png - Four equal small circles whose diameters partition a larger diameter]
  (1) \(28\pi\)
  (2) \(56\pi\)
  (3) \(14\pi\)
  (4) \(7\pi\)
  correct: 2
  solution: Each small diameter is one fourth of AB, so each small circumference is one fourth of the large circumference. Four such circumferences sum to the large circumference, 2π×7 = 14π.

  SAYS: So if you see a 90 and an α in both triangles — that's all you need. They're similar.
  --- slide 3: Opposite equal angles
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] [FIGURE: fig/v644.png - Similar right triangles, with one rotated relative to the other]
  [board] Matching sides $=$ opposite the same angle
  SAYS: Here are two similar triangles.
  (does: Mark the equal angles in both triangles)
  SAYS: Why similar? 90 and 90. The same α, the same β.
  SAYS: In similar triangles, the matching sides are the sides that sit opposite the same angles.
  SAYS: In a regular polygon there's no question of matching — every side is the same side.
  SAYS: In a triangle the sides are different: a short leg, a long leg, a hypotenuse.
  SAYS: So I have to be careful to compare the matching lines. How do I know which one matches?
  SAYS: I look at which angle the side is opposite.
  --- slide 4: Small–big table
  [on screen] Blank
