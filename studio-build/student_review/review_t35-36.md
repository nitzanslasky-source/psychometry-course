# Topic 35: Three-dimensional geometry

**Depends on:** Pythagoras and triples (3-4-5, 5-12-13), the "silver" 45-45-90 triangle (×√2), equilateral-triangle area a²√3/4, circle area and circumference, sector as a fraction of 360° (p16), the inscribed/circumscribed idea from circles (Q6, "the rule from circles"), areas of rectangle, triangle, trapezoid and parallelogram (Q3, p06, p08), ratio units (Q7), plugging in numbers, simplifying roots, dividing fractions (p15).
**Needed by:** T36 (the "Similar Solids" and "Volume Changes" lessons, Q13, p12, p16, p24, p27 all use cylinder and cone volumes). **Also:** T35 p27 (each dimension ×3/4, so volume ×27/64) uses the k³ rule, which is only taught in T36. Teach T35 first and move p27 to T36, or add one line on k³ to T35.

## 1. Coverage: what is missing

- **Liquid in containers (biggest gap).** NITE often asks things like: water is poured from container A into container B, so how high is it? Or: a cube is dropped into water, so how much does the level rise? The rule is: level height = volume ÷ base area, and the rise = object volume ÷ base area. Q1 only asks "can it hold the water". No lesson or practice item covers water level.
- **Units.** 1 L = 1000 cm³ = 1 dm³, 1 ml = 1 cm³, 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³. Slide 10 says "330 ml" but never links ml to cm³.
- **Counting faces, edges and vertices.** p07 asks for edges, but this is never taught. Add: a prism on an n-gon has 3n edges, 2n vertices and n+2 faces. A pyramid on an n-gon has 2n edges, n+1 vertices and n+1 faces.
- **Solids of revolution** (p01: a rectangle turned about a side makes a cylinder; a right triangle turned about a leg makes a cone, and which leg you use matters). Not taught.
- **Painted cube** (p24). Not taught. For an n×n×n cube: 8 cubes with 3 painted faces, 12(n−2) with 2, 6(n−2)² with 1, (n−2)³ with 0.
- **Slant height in a cone** (r, h, slant height form a right triangle, e.g. 3-4-5). This is a common exam step. Only the pyramid version is taught (Q8).
- **Cube nets / opposite faces of a die.** Appears on NITE sometimes. Not covered.
- **Formula page.** Tell students which 3D formulas are printed on the NITE formula page (check the current official page). Weak students then memorise less and panic less. Also say which facts are NOT on it (a√3, the cube angles, lost/gained faces).

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- Lesson 1, slides 2–3: "base = bottom or top face" and "lift a copy straight up". But the prism figure (v551) and Q4 and p05 show a prism **lying on its side**: the bases stand vertical and the "height 4" is a horizontal edge. A weak student will take AC (the vertical leg) as the height. Say it plainly: "Bases = the two identical faces. Height = the distance between them, even when the prism lies down."
- "Solids in Questions" is only a 0.6-min intro. All the key rules (a√2 and a√3, the cube angles, liquid vs. solid, lost/gained faces, most compact = smallest surface area) appear only inside solution videos. If a student skips one video, they miss a rule. Memory card 2 helps, but a 3-minute lesson video before Q9 would be safer.
- Q8–Q10 assume the silver triangle and Pythagoras are solid. Add one line: "silver = 45-45-90, the hypotenuse = leg × √2."
- Q10 slide 4 mnemonic "hamsa, bat mitzvah, bar mitzvah": fine for Israelis, but say "5, 12, 13" on the board too.

**Medium student**
- Good method habit: "straight or pointed?" and "ratio units". But the practice set uses many ideas that are never taught (p01, p07, p12, p16, p23, p24). These students will guess on them.
- Q9 slide 5 board: "Edge and a diagonal of **another** face → 90°". This is too loose. Edge AB and diagonal EG of the top face are on "another face", but the angle between them is 45° (skew lines). The memory card says "the face it stands on", which is better. Q10 slide 2 repeats "another face". Use one wording everywhere: "an edge and a face diagonal that meet at a corner, where the edge is perpendicular to that face → 90°."

**Strong student**
- Will like Q5 (compact shape), Q6 (a=1 fails), Q11 (cubes ≠ water) and Q12.
- Missing hard traps: water displacement; bricks (not cubes) in a box, where turning them changes the count; a cone filled to half its height holds only 1/8 of its volume (link to T36); the shortest path on the surface of a box (unfold the box).
- Q10 slide 3 trick "ignore the root, 18/3 = 6, attach the root": explain why in one line (18/√3 = 18√3/3), or strong students will misuse it with √2.

## 4. Methods and tricks to add

- **Water-level mini-lesson** (after Q1): "V = base area × height, so height = V ÷ base area." Two examples: pouring, and dropping in a cube.
- **Cube and box fact sheet** as a short video before Q9: a√2, a√3, box diagonal √(a²+b²+c²), the three cube angles (fixed wording), the n-gon prism/pyramid counts, and painted-cube counts.
- **Elimination by size** (from Q8) as a general habit: height < slant edge; a part < the whole; a fraction of a volume is between 0 and 1.
- **Units check before comparing** (cm³ vs. litres).

## 5. Practice

- 27 items with a good easy → hard spread (p01–p04 easy, p17–p23 hard). Most items use the taught methods (straight/pointed, ratios, inner triangles, lost/gained faces).
- **But** about 7 items depend on ideas that are never taught (see section 1). Either teach them or label them "challenge".
- No item on water level, units, or cone slant height. Add 3–4.
- There are few "which is bigger" / quantitative-comparison style items. Add 1–2.
- Not repetitive.

## 6. Verdict

- **Weak:** can do direct volume and surface questions and "straight vs. pointed". Will struggle with inner triangles and with the untaught practice items.
- **Medium:** will be reliable on most exam items, but will miss water-level and unit questions.
- **Strong:** will get good shortcuts, but still needs the hard traps (displacement, bricks, half-height cone).

**Top 3 changes**
1. Add a water-level / pouring / units mini-lesson with 3–4 practice items.
2. Add a short "cube and box facts" lesson video, and teach what the practice already uses (edge counts, solids of revolution, painted cube). Fix the "another face → 90°" wording.
3. Fix the prism base/height when the prism lies down (slides 2–3, Q4), the Q2 figure that gives the answer away, and the Q1 written solution.

# Topic 36: Similarity and scale

**Depends on:** ratios and ratio units, angles with parallel lines (corresponding and "Z" angles), vertical angles, triangle angle sum, Pythagoras and triples, the silver triangle (Q7), equilateral-triangle and regular-hexagon area (Q11), circle area, circumference and sectors (Q2, Q3, Q9, p08), inscribed angles on the same arc (p04), tangent ⊥ radius and tangent circles (p07, p10, p18), percent change (p20, "Volume Changes" slide 7), unit conversion cm→km (p23), cylinder and cone volumes from T35 (Q13, p12, p16, p24, p27).
**Needed by:** T35 p27 (the k³ rule). Later geometry and area questions that use "square the linear ratio".
**Note:** the midpoint segment (Q10) seems to be taught here for the first time. If an earlier topic teaches it, point back to that topic.

## 1. Coverage: what is missing

- **Map scale (the topic title says "scale"), a big gap.** No lesson teaches it. Only p23 tests it. Teach: scale 1:n means lengths ×n and areas ×n². Unit steps: cm→m (÷100), m→km (÷1000). Area units convert with the square (1 km² = 10¹⁰ cm²). This is a classic NITE item.
- **The part-to-part rule with a parallel line.** If DE ∥ BC, then AD:DB = AE:EC. Q6's shortcut uses exactly this (BD:DC = AE:EC). But the lesson (slide 7) and Q4 warn "don't match with the trapezoid leg". A weak student now sees two rules that seem to clash. State both clearly: "part:part on one side = part:part on the other side ✓. But DE:BC always uses the WHOLE side."
- **Trapezoid with its diagonals** (an hourglass inside a trapezoid). Bases a:b give the four triangles areas a² : ab : ab : b², and the two side triangles are equal. This is a frequent NITE question.
- **Same height means area ratio = base ratio, so do NOT square.** This is the most common wrong use of similarity. Add one contrast slide.
- **Altitude to the hypotenuse shortcuts:** h² = p·q and leg² = (its part) × hypotenuse. Q8 is exactly this. Put it on the memory card.
- **Percent change in area and volume** (sides +10% gives area +21% and volume +33.1%). Only p20 touches this.
- **"Not always similar":** rectangles, rhombi, isosceles triangles and right triangles are not automatically similar. One line on the regular-shapes slide.
- Spheres: Q12 uses them. Add "all spheres are similar" to the "Similar Solids" lesson.

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- The first two lessons are clear. The grid pictures (v628, v629, v632, v655) are very good.
- "Man with glasses" (slide 12) mixes three images (Kenny, Batman, binoculars). This is hard for non-native listeners. Keep one name.
- Q6 slide 5 "work across: AE/BD = 1.25, so EC = 8 × 1.25". It is correct, but 1.25 is not a similarity factor. This will confuse weak students. Drop it or explain it.
- Q6 slide 2: "Parallel lines, equal acute angles". Say "corresponding angles". The angles are not always acute.
- "From the course —" (slide 12 recap, "Volume Changes" slide 6) means nothing to a student. Rephrase.

**Medium student**
- The small–big table and "opposite the same angle" are strong methods.
- "Rectangle on the diagonal" (slide 11) has no numbers and no guided question. The line "top part to bottom part equals right part to left part" is vague. p17 needs this idea. Add one worked example.
- SSS/SAS appear only in the recap. That is fine for NITE.

**Strong student**
- Good shortcuts: area → root → cube (Q5), partial similarity (Q13), ratio units.
- Missing hard traps: trapezoid diagonals, "same height, don't square", map area, a cone filled to half its height (1/8), percent in volume.

## 3. Correctness and figures

- No wrong keys. All 13 guided and 27 practice answers check out.
- **Q3 video, slide 3: misleading exam advice.** It says: "In a harder question, the common mistake usually won't even be in the choices." NITE deliberately puts trap answers in hard questions too. Remove this, or change it to "a trap answer can be there at any level".
- **p19 solution wording:** "By similarity, AF is half AC = 14, hence AF = 7" reads as AF = 14. Write: "AC = 10 + 4 = 14, and AF is half of AC, so AF = 7."
- **Q5 figure (geo36-g142):** it shows a 4×4×4 cube, so a student can count 64 without using the area → edge step. Show two separate cubes.
- **v648 (altitude to hypotenuse):** the teacher uses 6, 8, 10 and gets 3.6 and 4.8, but the figure has no numbers. Add them.
- **v646 "Turn it around":** the figure is the same as v644, and nothing is turned. Show a rotated copy.
- **v632:** the figure has an empty description (alt text missing).
- Readability: geo36-g147 / v676, the label "1.4r" is cut by the inner circle. geo36-g150 / v689, "Small" sits on the dashed line. v631, "3 units" sits on the dashed line.
- v656: the radius labels "2" and "4" look like widths. Draw the radius segment.
- All other figures match their text and are drawn to scale. I checked the proportions in p01, p03, p09, p14, p18, p19, Q6 and Q7.

## 4. Methods and tricks to add

- **Map-scale mini-lesson** after "Similar Solids": 1 example for length, 1 for area, 1 unit-conversion ladder.
- **"When NOT to square"** slide: same height → base ratio. Only lengths change → linear ratio.
- **Trapezoid-diagonal slide** after the hourglass (slide 9): a², ab, ab, b².
- **Memory-card additions:** h² = p·q. Part:part = part:part with a parallel line. Percent → factor → power → back to percent.
- Plugging in (Q7, x = 3) and a plugged-in angle (Q8, 20°) are excellent. Make them a named habit in the recap.

## 5. Practice

- 27 items with a good spread. p05, p06 and p26 are easy. p09 and p14 are medium. p18, p19 and p24 are hard.
- They mostly use the taught methods (tables, area → length, ratio units, k³).
- p04 (inscribed angles) and p10 (tangent circles, not similarity) depend on the circles topic. Mark them or move them.
- Missing: map scale (only 1 item), trapezoid diagonals, "same height" contrast, percent change in volume.
- A little repetitive in "similar triangles/circles, find the area ratio" (p06, p08, p15, p21, p25). One of them could become a map-scale item.

## 6. Verdict

- **Weak:** can handle area/volume ratio questions well. Can be confused by the part:part vs. whole-side rules and by map scale.
- **Medium:** reliable on triangle similarity and ratios. Will miss map-scale, trapezoid-diagonal and "don't square" traps.
- **Strong:** fast on the core, but still needs the missing trap types.

**Top 3 changes**
1. Add a map-scale lesson (length, area, units) with 2–3 practice items. The topic title promises it.
2. Teach the parallel-line part:part rule explicitly so Q6 does not clash with slide 7 and Q4. Add trapezoid diagonals and "same height → don't square".
3. Remove the Q3 advice about traps not appearing in hard questions. Fix the Q5 figure that gives the answer away, add numbers to v648, and fix the p19 solution wording.
