# Topic 37: The coordinate system

**Depends on:** Pythagoras, triples, silver (45-45-90) and golden (30-60-90) triangles (T31). This is the core of the topic. Trapezoid, rhombus and square areas (T32: Q4, p01, p14). Circle area, circumference, sector, and tangent ⟂ radius (T33: Q2, Q6, p10, p23). Regular hexagon = 6 equilateral triangles, and the 30-30-120 triangle (T34: Q7, p11). Cylinder from a rotated rectangle (T35: Q5). Similar triangles (T36: Q3, Q8). Distance across zero (T17 number line, T13 absolute value). Dividing fractions (T3: Q3 choice 3). Roots: 6√2, √72 (T9–T10).
**Not taught anywhere:** the equation of a line. p25 ("2x + 3y = 12"), p26 ("the line y = −2") and the last line of the Q8 video ("y = 2/5 x + b") use it.
**Needed by:** T38 (p03's solution uses coordinates). Nothing else.

## 1. Coverage — what is missing

- **Quadrants.** "First quadrant" appears in Q2, Q3, p04 and p17, but the lesson never names the four quadrants or their signs. No topic in T30–T36 teaches them either. The exam asks: "(a, b) is in quadrant II. In which quadrant is (b, −a)?" Add one slide to video 1: a picture of the four quadrants with (+,+), (−,+), (−,−), (+,−). Add 2 practice questions.
- **Midpoint.** p22 needs it, but it is not taught. Add one line: "the midpoint is the average of the x values and the average of the y values". Also teach the "repeat the move" idea from p02 and p22 on the board.
- **Reflection across the axes / origin.** Only p21 has it, and it is not taught. Add a row to the memory card: x-axis → (x, −y), y-axis → (−x, y), origin → (−x, −y).
- **Line equations.** See above. Either teach a 2-slide "y = mx + b: m = steps, b = where the line cuts the y-axis; set x = 0 or y = 0 to find where the line cuts each axis", or remove p25, p26 and the Q8 equation remark.
- **Negative slope.** Every line in the lesson and in the practice goes up to the right. Add a "down-steps" example (e.g. from (0, 6), go 2 right and 3 down) and one practice question.
- **Area of a slanted triangle on the grid.** Put a rectangle around it and subtract the corner triangles. This is a classic exam type, and it is missing.

# Topic 38: Geometric reasoning

**Depends on:** Parallel lines, adjacent and complementary angles (T30: Q6, p04). From T31: triangle inequality (Q9, p23), exterior angle (Q10, p14), larger side ↔ larger angle (Q3), Pythagoras and the 9-12-15 triple (Q3, p25), isosceles (p01), same base same height. From T32: parallelogram adjacent angles and area = base × height (Q6, Q9, p13, p16), kite (p08), rhombus (p19), trapezoid (Q4). From T33: inscribed angle on a diameter = 90°, equal inscribed angles (slide 6, p24), chords and their distance from the center (Q8, p20, p21), tangent circles (p01). From T34: regular polygons (Q1, Q11). From T35: faces of prisms and pyramids (Q12). **Sphere (Q8): my search finds "sphere" only in T36, not in T35. Check that the sphere is taught before T38.** Coordinates (T37: p03's solution). Counting (T28: p15, p26). Min–max and "check the edges" (T21). Plugging in numbers (T18: Q13).
**Needed by:** nothing. This is the last geometry topic.

## 1. Coverage — what is missing

- **Two fixed sides, changing angle.** This is the most common exam idea here, and it is only half taught. Q9 says "the bigger the angle, the taller the height." That is true only up to 90°. Teach it fully: the area is greatest at 90°, and the angles θ and 180° − θ give the same area (e.g. a parallelogram with 60° and one with 120° have equal areas). Exam trap: "sides 5 and 8 — what is the greatest possible area?" (20). Also add the rule: "same two sides, bigger angle → longer third side".
- **Obtuse / right / acute from the sides** (c² vs a² + b²). This is used in Q3 and p25, but it appears only as a "check" in the Q3 video. Make it a rule slide.
- **Same base, same height between parallel lines** (the apex slides along a line and the area does not change). This is a classic flexible-diagram question, and it is missing from the lessons and the practice. It fits perfectly in "Diagrams That Can Change".
- **Shape efficiency with numbers:** circle vs square with the same perimeter (the ratio is 4 : π). There is no numeric shape-efficiency question.
- **3D reasoning:** there are no practice questions (only Q8 and Q12 in the guided part). Add: which cross-sections of a cube are possible (triangle, square, rectangle, pentagon, hexagon), and a shortest path on the surface of a box.

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- The order is confusing. The Q3 video ends with a goodbye ("that's the last of the geometry theory… What can I wish you… I'm waiting for you there too"). Then a new lesson, "Diagrams That Can Change", starts, and Q13 says goodbye again. Keep only the last goodbye.
- The **memory card comes before "Diagrams That Can Change"** and covers none of it. There is no must / could / cannot row, no "exaggerate the drawing, keep every given" row, and no "count what is fixed" row. This is half the topic's questions (Q4–Q13). Add those rows.
- Slide 3, "Move the vertex away": the rule works when the vertex moves straight away from the segment. Say so. A student may apply it to any "farther" point.

**Medium student**
- The flexible-drawing method (Q4, Q5) is very good. But the figures do not match the words (see Correctness), and that weakens the lesson.
- Q3 video, slide 3: "a vertex, and the segment opposite it gets longer — the angle opens" points back to slide 4, but slide 4 was a different situation (the vertex is fixed and the segment is extended). The rods picture ("two rods joined at α") is the right one. Teach it as its own rule.
- Q9 is hard to follow because of the error on slide 2 (see Correctness).

**Strong student**
- Good: "edges come from values β can't reach" (Q6), plugging in n = 3 rather than n = 2 (Q13), and the upper bound in Q12.
- Add for Q10: the angle-bisector ratio KN : NL = KM : ML, and KM > ML (the hypotenuse is longer), so KN > NL. This is one line, faster than the proof.

## 4. Methods and tricks to add

- **"Rods" rule** (a new slide after slide 4): two fixed sides; opening the angle → longer third side; the height (and area) grows up to 90°, then shrinks again.
- **"Slide the apex"** (Diagrams That Can Change): a triangle with a fixed base and its apex on a parallel line has constant area. Use Q4 as the example.
- **Extreme cases as the default test** for "must be true": push the free point to the end (a degenerate shape), to the middle (a symmetric shape), or to the center. This is already in Q4, Q5 and p17; name it as a 3-step checklist on the card.
- **"Cannot be determined" check:** find the free quantity (the angle in p16, the side in p02, the segment split in p08). If a given does not fix it, the answer is "cannot be determined". One line on the card.
