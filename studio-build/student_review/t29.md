### Q 1 (wp29-g147): A fair eight-sided dice is numbered 1 through 8. What is the probability of rolling an even number?
  (1) \(\frac38\)
  (2) \(\frac58\)
  (3) \(\frac12\)
  (4) \(\frac14\)
  correct: 2
  solution: The eight faces are equally likely. Four of them—two, four, six and eight—are even. Favourable outcomes divided by all outcomes is four eighths, which simplifies to one half. The numerator counts every result that satisfies the request, not the number of rolls.

### Q 2 (wp29-g148): A bag contains 5 green counters and 4 orange counters. One counter is chosen uniformly at random. What is the probability that it is green?
  (1) \(\frac59\)
  (2) \(\frac19\)
  (3) \(\frac49\)
  (4) \(\frac54\)
  correct: 0
  solution: There are nine individual counters, all equally likely to be drawn. Five of those are green, so the probability is five ninths. We are drawing one counter, but there are five different counters that would make the result successful. That is why the numerator is five rather than one.

### Q 4 (wp29-g150): A box contains 28 tokens, all either gold or silver. A uniformly random token is gold with probability \(\frac37\). How many silver tokens are in the box?
  (1) 18
  (2) 21
  (3) 16
  (4) 12
  correct: 2
  solution: The silver probability is \(1-\frac37=\frac47\). Since each token is equally likely, the silver share of the box is also four sevenths. One seventh of twenty-eight is four tokens, so four sevenths is sixteen.

### Q 6 (wp29-g153): A fair coin is tossed independently four times. What is the probability of tails on all four tosses?
  (1) \(\frac14\)
  (2) \(\frac18\)
  (3) \(\frac12\)
  (4) \(\frac1{16}\)
  correct: 3
  solution: Each toss has probability one half of tails, even after the earlier outcomes. We need tails on the first, second, third and fourth toss, so multiply four halves: \((\frac12)^4=\frac1{16}\).

### Q 7 (wp29-g154): A fair six-sided dice is rolled twice independently. What is the probability that the two results add to 8?
  (1) \(\frac4{36}\)
  (2) \(\frac7{36}\)
  (3) \(\frac5{36}\)
  (4) \(\frac16\)
  correct: 2
  solution: The successful pairs are (2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3) and (6,2). There are five. All six first results can pair with all six second results, so there are thirty-six equally likely ordered pairs. Five out of thirty-six gives the probability.
  solution: The pair (2,6) is different from (6,2) because the first and second rolls are distinct positions. The pair (4,4) is listed once, not twice: reversing it does not create a different ordered pair.

### Q 8 (wp29-g155): Rosa has tossed a fair coin and obtained tails six times in a row. Sam has tossed another fair coin and obtained heads six times in a row. All tosses are independent. How do their probabilities of heads on the next toss compare?
  (1) Sam’s probability is greater
  (2) The probabilities cannot be compared
  (3) They are equal
  (4) Rosa’s probability is greater
  correct: 2
  solution: Each next toss has a probability of one half of heads. Rosa’s previous tails do not make heads due, and Sam’s previous heads do not make another head less likely. The coins have no mechanism that remembers the earlier sequence and compensates for it. The probabilities are equal.

### Q 10 (wp29-g157): A bag contains 4 red counters and 4 yellow counters. Two are drawn uniformly at random without replacement. What is the probability that their colors differ?

  solution: The first counter can have either colour. After it is removed, seven counters remain. Exactly four have the opposite colour, whichever colour came first. The required second draw so has probability four sevenths.

### Q 11 (wp29-g158): A prize is equally likely to be in any of 7 closed lockers. You may open 2 different lockers, choosing without any extra information. What is the probability that you find the prize?
  (1) \(\frac27\)
  (2) \(\frac17\)
  (3) \(\frac13\)
  (4) \(\frac2{13}\)
  correct: 0
  solution: The two different lockers cover two of the seven equally likely prize locations. Therefore the chance is two sevenths. Choosing the second only after seeing the first empty does not improve or reduce this coverage, provided there is no extra information that favours a remaining locker.
