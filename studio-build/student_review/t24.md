  (does: Write S in 3 of the shaded boxes)

  SAYS: Now the maximum: give every sport player to a music student.
  (does: Write S in 7 shaded boxes)
  SAYS: Seven both. Can't go higher — there are only seven sport players.
  SAYS: So the overlap is somewhere from three to seven.
  --- slide 4: Max = smaller group
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] Maximum overlap = the smaller group
  SAYS: Now the technique — no drawing needed.
  SAYS: Put the small group inside the big one. That's the maximum.
  (does: Write max(8, 7) → 7)
  SAYS: Eight and seven: seven. Nothing to calculate.
  SAYS: Equal groups? Five and five — the maximum is five.
  --- slide 5: Min = the excess
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] Minimum overlap $=(A+B)-\text{total}$
  SAYS: The minimum: how far do the two groups go over the total?
  (does: Write 8 + 7 = 15)
  SAYS: Eight plus seven is fifteen.
  SAYS: But there are only twelve students.
  (does: Write 15 − 12 = 3)
  SAYS: Three too many. Those three have no choice — they must be both.
  SAYS: Picture fifteen items for twelve students: three students end up holding two.
  --- slide 6: When min is 0
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] $5+7=12$ students
  SAYS: What if the groups don't go over the total?
  SAYS: Five music, seven sport, twelve students. Five plus seven — exactly twelve.
  SAYS: Hand everything to different students. Nobody has to do both.
  (does: Next to it write "min = 0")
  SAYS: Minimum zero. Not minus anything.
  SAYS: If the sum doesn't pass the total, no overlap is forced.
  --- slide 7: Spot the wording
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] "at most" → the maximum possible
  [board] "at least" → the minimum that must be
  [board] "could be" · choices like "between 3 and 9" → a range
  SAYS: How do you recognise a range question?
  SAYS: "At most" — think MORE. That's the maximum.
  SAYS: "At least" — think LESS. The minimum that must be.
  SAYS: "What could be" — no single answer. And sometimes the choices themselves give it away.
  (does: Underline "most" and "least")
  SAYS: Spot the words — then go straight to max and min.
  --- slide 8: Worked example
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] 65% have property A · 55% have property B
  SAYS: Let's run both rules on one example.
  (does: Write max = 55%)
  SAYS: Max: the smaller group — fifty-five percent.
  (does: Write 65 + 55 − 100 = 20 → min = 20%)
  SAYS: Min: sixty-five plus fifty-five is a hundred twenty. Twenty percent over — so at least twenty percent have both.
  SAYS: Or reason it out: thirty-five percent don't have A. Give them B first.
  (does: Write 55 − 35 = 20)
  SAYS: Fifty-five minus thirty-five: twenty percent left, and they must double up. Same answer.
  SAYS: Formula or logic — use whichever feels natural. Most students find the formula — sum minus total — the easiest.

  SAYS: Two thirds plus a half is seven sixths — a sixth too many. At least a sixth are in both.
  (does: Next to it write "max = 1/2")
  SAYS: And the maximum? The smaller group — a half.

### Q 1 (wp24-g070): Of 45 campers, 28 bring a torch, 23 bring a map, and 16 bring both. How many bring neither?
  (1) 10
  (2) 6
  (3) 12
  (4) 16
  correct: 0
  solution: Sixteen are in the shared region. Map-only campers number twenty-three minus sixteen, or seven. The twenty-eight torch owners already include the shared region, so adding only the seven new map owners gives thirty-five with at least one item. Ten of the forty-five so have neither.
  solution: We could also find torch-only as twelve and add twelve, sixteen, and seven. That is valid, but it is not necessary if we already know the combined torch total.

  (1) 48
  (2) 84
  (3) 36
  (4) 24
  correct: 2
  solution: The group percentages add to 115%. The event has only 100% distinct guests, so at least fifteen percentage points represent people counted in both groups. Fifteen percent of 240 is twenty-four plus twelve, or thirty-six.

### Q 5 (wp24-g075): A class has 48 students. Twenty-one work in the greenhouse and eleven work in the orchard. Which could be the number who work in neither place?
  (1) 14
  (2) 30
  (3) 35
  (4) 18
  correct: 3
  solution: The smallest union is twenty-one, achieved if all eleven orchard workers also work in the greenhouse. That leaves at most twenty-seven in neither. The largest union is thirty-two, achieved if the groups are disjoint, leaving at least sixteen in neither.
  solution: So the neither count ranges from sixteen to twenty-seven. Eighteen is the only listed value inside this range. It can occur when the overlap is two: the union is 21+11−2=30, leaving eighteen.

### Q 6 (wp24-g076): Of a company’s employees, \(\frac25\) can code, \(\frac45\) work remotely, \(\frac15\) are juniors, and \(\frac12\) are in training. Which statement is not necessarily true?
  (1) At least one non-junior cannot code
  (2) At least one junior works remotely
  (3) At least one coder works remotely
  (4) At least one trainee cannot code
  correct: 1
  solution: Coders plus remote employees give \(\frac25+\frac45=\frac65>1\), forcing overlap. Trainees plus non-coders give \(\frac12+\frac35>1\), also forcing overlap. Non-juniors plus non-coders give \(\frac45+\frac35>1\), again forcing overlap.

### Q None (wp24-p01): At a fair, 150 different people each win one prize. Of the winners, 65% live nearby and 55% arrived by train. What is the minimum number who both live nearby and arrived by train?
  (1) 30
  (2) 15
  (3) 20
  (4) 45

  solution: The two group sizes add to 65%+55%=120% of all winners. Only 100% distinct people are available, so at least 20% were counted twice. Twenty percent of 150 is 30.

### Q None (wp24-p02): Every one of a group of students studies art, music, or both. There are 26 art students, 19 music students, and 11 who study both. How many students are in the group?
  (1) 56
  (2) 34
  (3) 30
  (4) 45
  correct: 1
  solution: Adding 26 and 19 counts the eleven students in both courses twice. Remove one copy of those eleven: 26+19−11=34.

### Q None (wp24-p05): In a town, \(\frac35\) of residents cycle, \(\frac14\) grow vegetables, \(\frac25\) keep pets, and \(\frac45\) are under 50. Which of the following overlaps necessarily contains at least one resident?

  (4) Cyclists who keep pets

### Q None (wp24-p06): Sixty trainees score between 0 and 12 (inclusive). Twenty-six score from 9 to 12 (inclusive), twenty-four from 7 to 10 (inclusive), and twenty score below 7. How many score from 9 to 10 (inclusive)?

  (3) 8
