## 1. Coverage: what is missing

Most important first.

- **"NOT" vs "not necessarily".** The Rectangle slide 3 says "NOT perpendicular · DON'T bisect the angles". The Parallelogram and Rhombus slides say "NOT equal". The properties card says the same. But Q15 teaches "every square is a rectangle", and a square's diagonals ARE perpendicular. A careful student sees a contradiction. The exam asks "which is necessarily true", so the wording matters. Change it everywhere to "**not necessarily** perpendicular / equal".
- **Trapezoid midsegment.** Midsegment = (a+b)/2, and area = midsegment × h. This is a common NITE item and it is missing. Add one board line on the Trapezoid slide 4.
- **Trapezoid diagonals: the two side triangles have equal area** (the "butterfly"). Classic NITE. It follows directly from the "same base, same height" principle in "Equal Heights, Equal Halves". Add it as slide 8 of that video, with one practice question.
- **Parallelogram with a 30°/45°/60° angle.** Example: sides 8 and 6 with a 30° angle → height 3 → area 24. This is very common and there is no guided example. Q11 comes close, but only for the rhombus.
- **Right trapezoid.** It is defined on slide 7 but no question uses it. Add one (drop one height → rectangle + right triangle).
- **Concave quadrilateral ("arrow").** Slide 3 shows it, but no rule and no question. Add: "the inside angle at the dent = the sum of the other three angles". Add one practice question.
- **Perimeter tricks are used in practice but never taught:** a cut adds 2 × its length to the total perimeter (fp01, adv-p15, adv-p24). The "staircase" rule: a stepped outline has the same perimeter as its bounding rectangle (adv-p14, adv-p19 are close). Add one slide after Q21.
- **Midpoint quadrilateral.** The midpoints of a rectangle's sides form a rhombus (fp16 gives this as data). The midpoints of a square's sides form a square with half the area (T33 needs it). One line on the properties card is enough.
- **Area = d₁·d₂/2 for ANY quadrilateral with perpendicular diagonals.** It is said only in passing in Q11 (slide 2), but adv-p27 tests it. Put it on the area card.
- **A bisector plus parallel sides gives an isosceles triangle.** Q9 and fp04 use it, but it is never named as a rule. Name it: "bisector in a parallelogram → isosceles triangle".

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- The "family" slide: the arrows go in mixed directions (square→rectangle is special→general, but parallelogram→rhombus is general→special). The student cannot tell "which shape is also which". Q15 and adv-p11 need exactly this. Add an inclusion tree: parallelogram → rectangle / rhombus → square, and kite → rhombus.
- The trapezoid definition "the legs aren't parallel" is incomplete. Say: "exactly one pair of parallel sides".
- The Kite slide 3 says "joins the two head angles". "Head angles" is never defined. Say "the angles between the equal sides (A and C)".
- Parallelogram slide 6: "height from D down to BC". In v194 the foot of this height falls outside BC, and nobody says so. Draw it from A, or say "extend BC".
- Q10 has three ways in 3 minutes, and Way 1 uses a height that falls outside the triangle. Q11 has six ways in 4 minutes. This is too much for a weak student. Put the simplest way first (Q10 Way 2, Q11 "two equilateral triangles") and label the others "optional".
- The fp02 solution uses "opposite angles of an isosceles trapezoid are supplementary". This is not taught. Add it to the isosceles-trapezoid slide (it follows from "base angles equal" + "same leg = 180").
- The golden and silver triangle names come from T31. Keep T31 right before T32.

**Medium student**
- The per-shape lessons are clear, and "drop both heights" and "name → properties" are good method rules.
- Q19 Approach 2 ("completions") ends with "it doesn't settle it here". That wastes a slide and leaves doubt. Either use a figure where completions work, or cut it.
- Q17 is a strong question. But the student only meets "which data is sufficient" once. Add one more such question in practice.
- fp24 and fp25 (percent change of area) are not taught here. Add one board line: "length ×k → area ×k²".

**Strong student**
- The six "one shape" videos repeat the memory cards. A strong student will skip them. That is fine: the cards are good.
- The advanced section is the right level (Q16–Q18 symmetry, Q20 plugging in, the Q21 3:2 shortcut). Missing hard traps: the trapezoid butterfly, the midsegment, concave angles, and "rectangle diagonal + perimeter → area" with the identity (adv-p21 appears only in practice; teach it once).
- Add "work back from the answers" for Q21-type questions: the area must be 24x², and the perimeter 50 fixes x. Also add unit-digit or estimation checks for area answers.

## 4. Figure problems

- **v224 (Right trapezoid slide) shows an isosceles trapezoid.** The teacher cannot "draw a leg perpendicular" on it. Use a real right trapezoid.
- **v219–v221 (generic trapezoid) are isosceles too.** Students will assume that every trapezoid is symmetric. Draw a non-isosceles one.
- **v165/v167 ("a general quadrilateral") are parallelograms** (opposite sides are equal and parallel). This works against "the sides don't have to be equal". Use an irregular shape.
- **g062 (Q13):** the "12" sits inside the thin triangle ABO, closer to AB than to BO. It can be read as AB = 12. Move it under BO.

- **adv-p12:** α at the top is at the crossing point, right next to the label A, so it looks like an angle at A. Move it or add a point label.

## 6. Verdict

- **Weak:** Mostly yes for direct shape questions. They will get stuck on "which is also which" and on "NOT vs not necessarily" until the family tree and wording are fixed.
- **Medium:** Yes for most exam questions. There are gaps on the trapezoid midsegment and diagonals, and on parallelograms with special angles.
- **Strong:** Yes. The advanced section is good. They still need the butterfly, the midsegment and the concave rule for the hardest items.
