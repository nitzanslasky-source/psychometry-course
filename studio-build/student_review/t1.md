### Q None (q-036): What is the product of the two smallest two-digit prime numbers?
  (1) \(121\)
  (2) \(143\)
  (3) \(169\)
  (4) \(131\)
  correct: 1
  solution: The two-digit primes begin 11, 13, 17, ... . The two smallest are 11 and 13, and their product is 11 · 13 = 143 . Note 121 = 11 · 11 uses the same prime twice, and 169 = 13 · 13 does too — the question asks for the two smallest distinct primes.

### Q None (q-037): Which statement about the number 1 is not correct?
  (1) It is an \(\text{odd}\) number
  (2) It is a prime number
  (3) It is a natural number
  (4) It is a divisor of every integer
  correct: 1
  solution: 1 is odd (true), 1 is a natural number (true), and 1 divides every integer (true). But 1 is not prime — a prime must have exactly two distinct divisors, 1 and itself, while 1 has only one divisor. So statement (2) is the false one.

### Q None (q-038): m and n are opposite numbers. \(m + n = ?\)
  (1) \(1\)
  (2) \(2\)
  (3) \(0\)
  (4) \(-1\)
  correct: 2
  solution: By definition, opposite numbers (additive inverses) sum to zero: if m = 9 then n = −9 , and 9 + (−9) = 0 . This holds for any pair of opposites.

### Q None (q-039): What will be the remainder if the smallest two-digit prime number is divided by 4?
  (1) \(3\)
  (2) \(1\)
  (3) \(2\)
  (4) \(0\)
  correct: 0
  solution: The smallest two-digit prime is 11. Dividing: 11 = 4 · 2 + 3 , so the remainder is 3.

### Q None (q-040): p and q are reciprocal numbers. \(p \cdot  q = ?\)
  (1) \(1\)
  (2) \(0\)
  (3) \(-1\)
  (4) \(2\)
  correct: 0
  solution: By definition, reciprocal numbers (multiplicative inverses) multiply to one: if p = 5 then q = 1:5 , and 5 · 1:5 = 1 . This holds for every reciprocal pair.
