### Q 1 (wp27-g107): A small cart moves at 4 meters per second. How many kilometers does it travel in 25 minutes?
  (1) 1
  (2) 5
  (3) 10
  (4) 6
  correct: 3
  solution: In one minute there are sixty seconds, so the cart covers 4×60=240 metres. Twenty-five minutes give twenty-five groups of 240 metres: 6,000 metres. A kilometre is one thousand metres, so that is six kilometres. We changed seconds to minutes before using the twenty-five-minute interval.

### Q 2 (wp27-g108): A drone climbs along a straight line at 390 kph. After 2 minutes, its horizontal displacement is 12 kilometers. How high above its starting level is it, in kilometers?
  (1) 12
  (2) 13
  (3) 5
  (4) 6
  correct: 2
  solution: Two minutes is one thirtieth of an hour. At 390 kilometres per hour, the drone travels thirteen kilometres along its sloping path. Draw a right triangle: twelve is the horizontal side, the unknown height is the vertical side, and thirteen is the sloping side opposite the right angle.
  solution: By Pythagoras, the height squared is thirteen squared minus twelve squared: 169−144=25. The height is five kilometres. Thirteen is the length of the flight path, so it is not the requested height.

### Q 3 (wp27-g109): A shuttle travels from a station to a hotel at 72 kph and returns along the same route at 48 kph, without stopping. What is its average speed for the whole round trip?
  (1) 64 kph
  (2) 57.6 kph
  (3) 60 kph
  (4) 54 kph
  correct: 1
  solution: The one-way distance is not given, and its size will cancel. Choose 144 kilometres because both speeds divide it neatly. At seventy-two kilometres per hour the outward trip takes two hours. At forty-eight it takes three hours. The total is 288 kilometres in five hours, giving 57.6 kilometres per hour.
  solution: Average speed means total distance divided by total elapsed time. It is the one steady speed that would cover the whole route in the same overall time. It does not usually mean adding the two listed speeds and dividing by two. Here the slower speed lasts three hours while the faster one lasts only two.
  solution: This also explains why the answer is below sixty, the midpoint of the two speeds. More time is spent at forty-eight, so that speed has more influence on the time-weighted average. The simple average of sixty would be correct for equal times at the two speeds, but these journeys have equal distances.

### Q 4 (wp27-g111): Noor and Eli start at the same time from opposite ends of a path and walk towards one another. Eli walks at 1.75 times Noor’s speed. What fraction of the path does Noor cover before they meet?
  (1) \(\frac14\)
  (2) \(\frac12\)
  (3) \(\frac7{11}\)
  (4) \(\frac4{11}\)
  correct: 3
  solution: Noor’s and Eli’s speed ratio is 1:1.75. Multiply both entries by four to make whole-number units: 4:7. They start together and we stop measuring at the same meeting, so they walk for equal amounts of time. Their distances so have that same ratio, four to seven.
  solution: The full path consists of Noor’s four distance units plus Eli’s seven. That makes eleven in all. Noor covers four of those eleven, giving four elevenths. The denominator is eleven because the question asks for Noor’s share of the whole path, not Noor’s distance compared only with Eli’s distance.
  solution: Imagine dividing the path into eleven equal strips. Noor walks across four strips from one end while Eli walks across seven from the other. Both arrive at the same dividing point. The strips represent relative distances; we are not claiming they are one metre long. Any common strip length leads to the same fraction.

### Q 5 (wp27-g112): Two boats leave opposite ends of a canal at the same time. Boat B is 3.5 times as fast as boat A. By the time B has traveled the full length of the canal, what fraction of that length has A traveled?
  (1) \(\frac27\)
  (2) \(\frac29\)
  (3) \(\frac57\)
  (4) \(\frac79\)
  correct: 0
  solution: The speed ratio A:B is 1:3.5, or 2:7. Over the same time, A covers two distance units while B covers seven. This time, B’s seven units alone represent the whole canal, because the question ends when B reaches the far end. A so covers two sevenths.
  solution: We do not add two and seven to make nine. That would describe their combined distances up to a meeting between them. Here the endpoint is B finishing its own full journey. Drawing the starting points is useful, but identifying when the clock stops is what tells us which distance is the whole.

### Q 6 (wp27-g114): Two walkers leave the same gate at the same time in opposite directions along a straight path. Their speeds are 5 and 7 kph. After how many minutes are they 800 meters apart?
  (1) 2
  (2) 3
  (3) 5
  (4) 4
  correct: 3
  solution: Every hour, one walker adds five kilometres to one side of the gap and the other adds seven to the other side. Their separation so grows at twelve kilometres per hour. The requested separation is eight hundred metres, or 0.8 kilometres.
  solution: Time is distance divided by relative speed: \(\frac{0.8}{12}=\frac1{15}\) of an hour. One fifteenth of sixty minutes is four minutes. We add the speeds here because the walkers move away from each other in opposite directions.

### Q 7 (wp27-g115): At 08:00 a van leaves Pine for Lake at 60 kph. At 08:30 a car leaves Lake for Pine at 90 kph. The towns are 105 kilometers apart. When do they meet?
  (1) 08:42
  (2) 09:12
  (3) 09:30
  (4) 09:00
  correct: 3
  solution: During the first half hour only the van moves. At sixty kilometres per hour it covers thirty kilometres. At 08:30, when the car starts, the remaining gap is 105−30=75 kilometres.
  solution: From then on they move towards each other, so the gap closes at 60+90=150 kilometres per hour. A seventy-five-kilometre gap takes half an hour to close at that rate. Half an hour after 08:30 is 09:00.
  solution: The relative-speed calculation begins when both are moving. Using the original 105-kilometre distance with the combined speed would incorrectly give the car credit for travelling before it actually left.
