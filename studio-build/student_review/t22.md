  [on screen] Blank

  [board] A is 9 less than B:  $A=B-9$

  SAYS: A is six more than B. Start with B, add six — you reach A. A equals B plus six.
  SAYS: A is nine less than B. Start with B, take away nine. A equals B minus nine.
  (does: Under the second line write "B = A + 9")
  SAYS: Same fact, the other way round: B is A plus nine. Both are right — just write it the right way.
  SAYS: Quick check: if B is twenty, A is eleven. Not negative eleven.
  --- slide 5: Times and twice
  [on screen] Blank

  [board] Twice / double $=$ always $\times2$

  SAYS: Don't mix up three TIMES as much with three MORE.
  (does: Next to A = 3B write "B = 7 → 21, not 10")
  SAYS: If B is seven: three times is twenty-one. Three more would be ten.
  SAYS: And "twice" or "double" — always times two. Just like "half" is always divided by two.
  SAYS: Three times, four times — those get their own number. Twice is only ever two.
  --- slide 6: Fractions of a whole
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] Two fifths of $n$:  $\frac25n$
  [board] One quarter less than $n$:  $\frac34n$

  SAYS: One quarter less than n: take all of n, remove a quarter of it — three quarters of n are left.
  (does: Under it write "n − ¼n = ¾n")
  SAYS: The quarter is a quarter OF n. n is the whole.

  [on screen] Blank

  SAYS: We don't always know which one is bigger. That's why it's written with absolute-value bars — a difference is always positive.
  (does: Under it write "A − B = 12  or  B − A = 12")
  SAYS: If A is bigger, A minus B is twelve. If B is bigger, B minus A is twelve.
  SAYS: When the story tells you who's bigger — the older person, say — just write bigger minus smaller.
  --- slide 8: Makes up · costs as

  SAYS: Three notebooks: three N. Five pens: five P. Three N equals five P.
  (does: Circle "the same as" and write "=" above it)

  --- slide 3: Triangle value
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] A student solves 15 exercises every 6 minutes. How many does he solve in 8 minutes?
  SAYS: Now a harder one. Fifteen exercises in six minutes. How many in eight?
  SAYS: Across: six to fifteen — times two and a half. Down: six to eight — not whole either.
  SAYS: So we use the triangle value.
  (does: Draw a diagonal line from 8 to 15)
  SAYS: Multiply along the diagonal: eight times fifteen.
  (does: Write "8 × 15 : 6")

  SAYS: When ratios are equal, the diagonal products are equal.

  SAYS: Six x equals eight times fifteen.
  (does: Write "6x = 120 → x = 20")
  SAYS: A hundred and twenty. x is twenty. Same answer.
  SAYS: Triangle value or cross-multiplying — pick whichever feels natural.
  SAYS: I prefer the triangle value: it writes the answer straight away, without isolating x.

### Q 1 (wp22-g029): Six trays hold 42 biscuits. At the same number per tray, how many biscuits fit on 15 trays?
  (1) 112
  (2) 105
  (3) 90
  (4) 98
  correct: 1
  solution: Forty-two biscuits are shared equally among six trays, so each tray holds seven. Fifteen trays hold fifteen groups of seven, which is 105. Finding the value for one tray is a dependable route whenever a direct multiplier is not immediately comfortable.

### Q 2 (wp22-g030): At a constant pace, a student completes 12 exercises in 8 minutes. How many exercises does she complete in 14 minutes?
  (1) 24
  (2) 21
  (3) 18
  (4) 20
  correct: 1
  solution: Twelve exercises in eight minutes means three exercises in two minutes: divide both numbers by four. Fourteen minutes contains seven two-minute blocks, so the student completes seven groups of three, or twenty-one exercises. This avoids an awkward one-minute rate if you prefer whole numbers.

### Q 3 (wp22-g032): A bakery sells a tart for 58 credits. Making it uses 300 g of pastry at 6 credits per 100 g, 200 g of filling at 9 credits per 100 g, and 4 berries at half a credit each. Ignoring other costs, what is the selling price minus ingredient cost?
  (1) 18
  (2) 22
  (3) 38
  (4) 20
  correct: 3
  solution: The pastry uses three 100-gram portions, costing eighteen credits. The filling uses two such portions, also costing eighteen. Four berries at half a credit each cost two credits. The total ingredient cost is eighteen plus eighteen plus two, or thirty-eight.
  solution: The requested difference is the selling price minus that cost: fifty-eight minus thirty-eight is twenty. Thirty-eight is a useful intermediate value, but it is not what the question asks us to select.

### Q 4 (wp22-g033): Hire company A charges 3 credits for every minute or part of a minute. Company B charges 14 credits for every 5-minute block or part of a block. What is the difference between the charges for an 11.5-minute hire?
  (1) 9
  (2) 6
  (3) 3
  (4) 7.5
  correct: 1
  solution: Company A charges for twelve minutes because the final half-minute starts a new minute. Its charge is twelve times three, or thirty-six. Company B charges for three five-minute blocks, because two blocks cover only ten minutes. Its charge is three times fourteen, or forty-two.
  solution: The difference is six credits. We round the number of billing units up; we do not round 11.5 to the nearest five or charge a fraction of the last block. “Or part of” is the wording that forces this.

  SAYS: Ratios show up across the whole quantitative section — side ratios in geometry, rates in work and motion problems.

  --- slide 2: What a ratio tells you
  [on screen] Blank
  SAYS: In everyday talk, "he's tall compared to me" only says he's taller. Not by how much.
  SAYS: In maths, a ratio tells you exactly how many times.
  SAYS: The ratio of blue to orange is three to five. For every three blue, there are five orange.
  SAYS: So there's more orange — and not just more: five thirds as much. One and two thirds times.
  --- slide 3: A ratio is a fraction
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] Blue : orange $=3:5=\frac35$
  [board] $12:20=3:5$
  SAYS: A ratio is just a fraction written with a colon. Three to five is three fifths — three divided by five.
  SAYS: Read it in order: the first name goes with the first number. Blue three, orange five.
  SAYS: And don't worry about being tricked on the order — the exam avoids that wording. When it does appear, it's obvious which is which: the older child gets the bigger number.
  (does: Write ":4" under both 12 and 20)
  SAYS: And because it's a fraction, you can expand it or reduce it. Twelve to twenty is three to five.
  SAYS: Usually we write a ratio fully reduced — like a fraction.
  --- slide 4: No amounts!
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] $3:5$ could be $3\ \&\ 5$,  $6\ \&\ 10$,  $30\ \&\ 50$, …
  [board] Blue $=3x$,  orange $=5x$
  SAYS: The big one:
  SAYS: From a ratio alone, you can't know the amounts. Three and five? Six and ten? Thirty and fifty? Only the relationship.
  SAYS: You'll know the amounts only when they give you an actual number — a total, a difference, or one of the groups.
  SAYS: That's why we write the amounts with x: three x and five x.
  (does: Write "3x/5x = 3/5" and cross out both x's)
  SAYS: Cancel the x and the ratio is still three to five — but now we can build equations with it.
  --- slide 5: Part of a whole
  [on screen] Blank
  [board] Blue is $\frac38$ of all the items
  SAYS: The same idea hides in different wording.
  SAYS: Blue is three eighths of all the items. Three out of eight.
  (does: Write "blue = 3x,  all = 8x")
  SAYS: So blue is three x — and ALL of them is eight x.
  (does: Write "orange = 8x − 3x = 5x")
  SAYS: Orange is whatever's left: five x.
  --- slide 6: Fractions in a ratio
  [on screen] Blank

  SAYS: Sometimes the ratio has a fraction or a decimal: one and a half to two.

### Q 5 (wp22-g035): In a class, boys make up \(\frac38\) of all students. Which could be the difference between the numbers of girls and boys?
  (1) 15
  (2) 17
  (3) 21
  (4) 18
  correct: 3
  solution: Boys occupy three parts out of eight. Girls occupy the remaining five. The difference is two parts, so it must be twice a whole-number unit count and so even. Only eighteen among the choices is even.
  solution: To confirm that eighteen is possible, take nine students per part. Then there are twenty-seven boys and forty-five girls, a difference of eighteen, and twenty-seven is three eighths of the total seventy-two.
