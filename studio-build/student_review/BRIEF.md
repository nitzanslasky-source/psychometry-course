# Math topic review — as a student

You are reviewing two topics of an English prep course for the Israeli psychometric exam (NITE), quantitative section.
The course is for first-time takers. Read the files `t<N>.md` in this folder: they show, in order, exactly what a student
gets — each video lesson (slide titles, what is written on the board `[board]`, what the teacher says `SAYS:`, what the
teacher draws `(does: …)`), memory cards, then guided questions with solutions and a practice set.

Go through each topic three times, honestly, as three different students:
- **Weak student**: shaky basics, forgets rules, gets lost when steps are skipped.
- **Medium student**: follows well, needs clear methods and practice to become reliable.
- **Strong student**: learns fast, wants shortcuts and speed, is bored by over-explanation, needs the hard exam traps.

Then answer as the course's reviewer. Be concrete — quote the slide/question id.

1. **Coverage.** Does the topic teach everything the real exam asks on this subject? List any exam question type,
   rule or trap that is missing or only mentioned in passing. (You know the NITE quantitative section; be specific.)
2. **Teaching quality per level.** For weak / medium / strong: where would they get lost, bored, or leave with a gap?
   Steps skipped, explanations too fast or too long, an idea used before it is taught, unclear wording.
3. **Correctness.** Any mathematical error, wrong or ambiguous question, wrong key, or solution that doesn't match its
   question (check every practice question quickly; solve the ones that look suspicious).
4. **Methods and tricks you would add or teach differently.** Shortcuts (plugging in numbers, working back from the
   answers, estimation, elimination, symmetry, choosing easy values, unit digits, etc.), a better order, a clearer
   explanation, a visual. Say where it would go and roughly what it would say.
5. **Practice.** Enough questions? Right spread of difficulty (easy → exam-hard)? Do the practice questions actually
   use the methods taught? Anything repetitive?
6. **Verdict.** Would each level come out able to solve this topic's exam questions? One line each, and the top 3
   changes that would help most, in priority order.

Write your report to `review_t<A>-<B>.md` in this folder (plain language, headings per topic, bullets; the teacher is not
a native English speaker, so keep sentences short). Do not edit any course file. Keep the report under ~1,200 words
per topic; put the most important points first.

Note: the order in which students learn the topics may still change. When a topic uses a rule taught elsewhere,
list it as a DEPENDENCY ("needs: exponent laws from T8") rather than only as an error, so the teacher can plan the
order. Add a short "Depends on / Needed by" line at the top of each topic's review.
Solution text in the t<N>.md files is exactly what students see (the dump's earlier "<"/">" loss is fixed).

Figures (geometry): lines like `[FIGURE: fig/NAME.png - description]` point to images in the `fig/` folder. Open them
with your image-reading tool when the figure matters (every guided question, every practice question you check, and
lesson slides that teach from a picture). Check that each figure matches its question (labels, marked angles, given
lengths, nothing that gives away or contradicts the answer, not misleadingly drawn unless the question says "not to
scale"), and that the lesson figures actually show what the teacher says.
