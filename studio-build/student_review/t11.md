### Q 1 (q-288): Given: \(x \ne  0\). \(\frac{{\left(3\,x\right)}^{4} \cdot  {\left(5\,x\right)}^{3}}{{\left(15\,{x}^{2}\right)}^{3}} \cdot  \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)x = ?\)
  (1) \(\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)x\)
  (2) \(3\,{x}^{2}\)
  (3) \(\frac{5\,x}{3}\)
  (4) \({x}^{2}\)
  correct: 3
  solution: Expand each power: (3x)⁴ = 81x⁴ and (5x)³ = 125x³ , so the numerator is 81 · 125 · x⁷ = 10125x⁷ . The denominator is (15x²)³ = 3375x⁶ . Dividing: 10125:3375 = 3 and x⁷:x⁶ = x , so the fraction is 3x . Finally 3x · (1:3)x = x² .

### Q 2 (q-289): Given: a, b, c and d are nonzero. \(\frac{{a}^{-2} \cdot  {b}^{3}}{{c}^{4} \cdot  {d}^{-5}} = ?\)
  (1) \(\frac{{d}^{5} \cdot  {b}^{3}}{{c}^{4} \cdot  {a}^{2}}\)
  (2) \(\frac{{a}^{2} \cdot  {b}^{3}}{{c}^{4} \cdot  {d}^{5}}\)
  (3) \(\frac{1}{{a}^{2} \cdot  {b}^{3} \cdot  {c}^{4} \cdot  {d}^{5}}\)
  (4) \(\frac{{c}^{4} \cdot  {b}^{3}}{{d}^{5} \cdot  {a}^{2}}\)

  solution: A negative exponent moves a factor across the fraction bar with a positive exponent: a⁻² upstairs becomes a² downstairs, and d⁻⁵ downstairs becomes d⁵ upstairs. The positive-exponent factors b³ and c⁴ stay put, giving (b³ · d⁵):(a² · c⁴) .

### Q 3 (q-290): Let \(x\) be real and \(y\) be an integer, with \(x \ne 1\) and \(x^{y+2} = 1\). Which claim is necessarily true?
  (1) \(x = 0\) or \(y \ne  -2\)
  (2) \(y = -2\) or \(x = -1\)
  (3) \(x = 0\) or \(y \ne  0\)
  (4) \(x = 0\)
  correct: 1
  solution: A power equals 1 in only three ways: the base is 1 (excluded here), the exponent is 0, or the base is −1 with an even exponent. So either y + 2 = 0 , i.e. y = −2 , or x = −1 (with y + 2 even). In every possible case, at least one of " y = −2 " or " x = −1 " holds, so claim (2) is necessarily true.

### Q 4 (q-291): Given: \(1 < x\), y. \({x}^{y} = {y}^{x}\) and \(x = {y}^{2}\). \(y = ?\)
  (1) \(6\)
  (2) \(2\)
  (3) \(3\)
  (4) \(4\)
  correct: 1
  solution: Substitute x = y² into x^y = y^x : (y²)^y = y^(y²) , so y^(2y) = y^(y²) . With y > 1 the exponents must match: 2y = y² , so y² − 2y = 0 and y(y − 2) = 0 , giving y = 2 ( y = 0 is excluded). Check: x = 4 , and 4² = 16 = 2⁴ ✓.

### Q 5 (q-292): For real \(x\) and \(y\), \(5^x \cdot 5^y = 125\) and \(2xy = 4\). What is \(x^2 + y^2\)?

  (2) \(0\)
  (3) \(3\)
  (4) \(4\)

### Q 6 (q-293): Given: \({2}^{x} = {2}^{y} + {2}^{y} + {2}^{y} + {2}^{y}\). \(x - y = ?\)
