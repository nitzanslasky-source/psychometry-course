# Topic 23: Percentages

**Depends on:** fractions: reduce, expand, compare same-top fractions (T2–T3: slides 3–4, Q2, Q4, p01); linear equations with brackets (T6–T7: slide 5 says "go back to the equations lessons"); ratio table / triangle value and ratio units (T22: slide 6, Q9, p11, p14, p18); letters in expressions (T4–T5, T18: p16, p18, p20); prime factors (T14: p03 shortcut below).
**Needed by:** T24 (Q4, Q6 method 2, p01, p07, p15 are all in percent); T25 Averages (weighted percents); T26 Work and rates (growth); T29 Probability (the percent tree = probability tree). T22 before T23 is the right order.

## 1. Coverage — what is missing

- **The multiplier method is used but never taught.** p03 ("×1.5"), p10 ("1.1×1.1=1.21"), p24 ("160×0.80×0.85"), Q10 ("0.8×0.8") and the Q5 video ("1.3 × 0.75x") all use it. No slide says "+20% = ×1.2, −15% = ×0.85, two changes = multiply the factors". This is the fastest tool for repeated changes. Add one slide to video 2, after slide 3.
- **Percent vs. percentage points.** A classic exam trap: "the rate went from 20% to 25%" = 5 percentage points, but a 25% increase. Missing completely. Add one slide and one practice question.
- **Change formula as a named rule.** "Change ÷ original × 100" is only in slide 8 ("From 80 to 100"), in one sentence. It is the base of Q4, p13, p17, p25, p26. Put it on the board and on the memory card.
- **"150% of" vs "50% more than".** A common exam wording trap. Not mentioned.
- **Same percent up and down.** p22 (+20% then −20% → −4%) is practised but the rule is not taught: up p% then down p% (either order) always gives a loss of p²/100 %. The strong student wants this.
- **Mixtures.** p23 (salt and water) uses a method no lesson teaches ("the salt stays, the total changes"). No other topic teaches mixtures either. Teach it on one slide here, or remove p23.
- **Percent questions with only letters** (p16, p18, p20, answer choices in x). Only slide 5 shows letters. Add one guided question, and teach "plug in numbers for the letters too" (e.g. p20: x = 64 → original 100 → 36%; check which choice gives 36).

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- Slide 6 calls the method "the triple value". T22 calls the same method "the triangle value". Use one name. Also, the board shows no ratio table — only "(does: draw the diagonal)". The weak student needs to see the two columns (percent | amount) drawn.
- Slide 4 goes through 15 conversions in one breath. Split it: first ½, ¼, ⅛, 1/10, 1/5, 1/20; then the thirds and the "families".
- Slide 4: "A sixth… Can't halve it precisely? Half of thirty-two is sixteen — so it's sixteen and a bit." This is confusing — you CAN halve 33⅓ exactly. Say: "half of 33 is 16½, half of ⅓ is ⅙ → 16⅔."
- Solutions use ":" for division: p08 "70:200", p17 "66:240", p23 "60:0.15=400". Dividing by a decimal with ":" is hard to read. Use a fraction bar, and in p23 say "60 = 15% → 20 = 5% → 400 = 100%".
- p27: "Divide 306 by 85 to get 3.60" is hard mental math. Better: 85% = 306 → 5% = 18 → 100% = 360. Or test the round answer: 360 × 0.85 = 306.
- The memory card (after video 1) already lists "Plug in 100" and "Percent tree", which video 2 has not taught yet. Move the card after video 2.

**Medium student**
- Q8 method 2 ("percent × k ⟺ whole ÷ k") skips choice 1 (10% of 6x). Show it: percent × ⅔, whole × 3/2.
- Q7 video slide 3: "A percent of a number equals that number percent of the percent" is a tongue-twister. Say: "a% of b = b% of a — you can swap them."
- Slide 5 (x inside the percent) comes before any easy guided question with a letter. Fine for medium, too fast for weak — mark it "exam level".

**Strong student**
- Video 1 slides 1–3 are slow for them. Fine, it is short.
- They want: the multiplier method, the successive-change formula (a + b + ab/100: +25, −20 → 25 − 20 − 5 = 0), the p²/100 rule, and percentage points.
- p03: the solution computes six prices up to 1,093.5. Shortcut: 96 = 2⁵·3. Each ×3/2 uses one factor 2. Five 2's → five reviews stay whole, the sixth does not. Teach this in the solution.

## 4. Methods and tricks to add

- **Multiplier slide** (video 2, after slide 3): "+x% → × (1 + x/100); −x% → × (1 − x/100). Two changes → multiply." Example: +10% twice → ×1.21 → +21%.
- **Test the answers backwards** (already in Q10 method 2). Make it a named method on the memory card. It works for p05, p10, p27.
- **Plug in numbers for letter answers** (p16, p18, p20): choose N = 10, b = 4 → English = 1.6 → b²/N = 1.6.
- **Estimation / elimination:** p17 needs only "66 is a bit more than ¼ of 240 (60)" → between 25% and 30%. Show this.
- **Percentage points** slide, with one example in a table of rates.

- **Strong:** will finish fast but is missing the multiplier / successive-change shortcuts and the hardest wording traps.

3. Fix "triple value" → "triangle value", draw the ratio table on the board, and teach (or remove) the mixture question p23.

# Topic 24: Overlapping groups

**Depends on:** percentages and complements (T23: Q4, Q6 method 2, p01, p07, p15); fractions with common denominators (T2–T3: Q6, Q7, p08, p10); ratio units (T22: Q3, slide 6 of "Exact Overlap").
**Needed by:** T28 Counting (the "no overlap — don't count twice" rule, t28 slide ~479); T29 Probability ("at least one", complements, A or B). T23 before T24 is necessary.

## 1. Coverage — what is missing

- **Three groups.** p13 (reading fiction, history, science — "minimum who read all three") is not taught anywhere. Its solution ends with "zero may be possible. We still need a construction to confirm it." — the student is left without an answer. Either remove p13 or teach: min(all three) = A + B + C − 2·total (or 0). Construction for p13: 35 read fiction+history, 30 fiction+science, 30 history+science, 5 fiction only → 70, 65, 60, total 100, nobody reads all three. Put that in the solution.
- **Two-way table (2×2).** A very common exam type: two yes/no traits of the same people, e.g. "60% are women; 30% of the women and 20% of the men wear glasses…". The strip does not handle "percent of a sub-group" well. A 2×2 table with totals is the standard tool. Add one slide and 2 questions.
- **Min/max of other regions.** The lessons teach only min/max of "both". Exams also ask min/max of neither, of union, of "A only" (Q5, p07, p16 ask these). Add a board: "neither = total − union; union goes from the larger group to min(A+B, total). A only = A − both → min A only uses MAX both."
- **"Exactly one"** appears only on the last recap board. p12 and p17 ask it. Give it one sentence with an example: exactly one = union − both = A + B − 2·both.

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- Video 1 moves from circles (Venn) to the formula to "two types" in 2.4 minutes, then video 3 switches to a strip. Say clearly on screen that the strip is the circles "unrolled": draw the Venn, then cut it into the four boxes.
- Slide 7 of "Overlap Ranges": "'At most' → think MORE. That's the maximum. 'At least' → the minimum." This is dangerous. The word goes with the thing asked, not with "both". p16 asks "At least how many round badges are NOT metal" — the answer uses the MAXIMUM overlap (48 − 30 = 18). p07 asks the greatest "neither", which uses the SMALLEST union. Change the board to: "at most / at least tells you max or min of the thing they ask. Ask yourself: to make THIS small, what must the overlap be?"
- Q5 method 1 (neither as the overlap of two complements) is a big jump. Add a simpler route first: neither = 48 − union; union from 21 to 32 → neither 16 to 27.
- Q2 method 1 (neither + the whole polish bracket) is a roundabout path. Method 2 (19, then 26 − 19) is easier; show it first.

**Medium student**
- Q4 method 3 (the range seen on the strip) is excellent — keep.
- Q6: good. The "10 employees" picture proving "exactly 1 is not forced" is very clear.

**Strong student**
- Well served by Q4 method 2, Q5 method 2 (elimination), Q6 in percent.
- They want: three-group min, the 2×2 table, the "neither" range formula, and "at least one of three" traps.

## 3. Correctness

- **Board error, "Overlap Ranges" slide 4:** "(does: Write max(8, 7) → 7)". max(8, 7) = 8. It should be "min(8, 7) → 7" or "smaller of 8 and 7 → 7". A student who knows the notation will be confused.
- **Q8 (wp24-g078) wording:** "Two researchers photograph **different** butterflies" can be read as "they photograph different (not the same) butterflies" — that means no overlap, which contradicts the answer 13. Write "Two researchers photograph butterflies."
- **p13 solution** is incomplete (see Coverage). The key (0) is right, but the solution does not prove it.
- **Q5 on-screen text:** "Twenty−one" is shown with a math minus sign instead of a hyphen (Q5 video, both slides). Display bug.
- All other keys and arithmetic check: Q4 method 3 strips add to 100% (15+20+65; 20+15+60+5; 35+0+45+20); p06 union 7–12 has 40 → both 10; p10 union 11/12 ≤ 1, so data is consistent.

## 4. Methods and tricks to add

- **Union range line** (Overlap Ranges, after slide 6): "larger group ≤ union ≤ min(A+B, total)". This solves Q5, p07 in one line.
- **Work in percent, not counts** — already in Q4 method 2 and Q6 method 2. Make it a named tip on the memory card.
- **2×2 table** slide in "Exact Overlap": rows A / not A, columns B / not B, with totals. Same sudoku idea as the strip.
- **"Given data = total" check:** in p15 (55% + 45% = 100%), tell the student that adding to exactly 100% with 20% neither still forces a 20% overlap — a nice contrast with Q6.

## 5. Practice

- 8 guided + 17 practice: enough in number, a bit thin for the medium student on range questions about neither/union (only p07).
- Spread: easy-heavy (p02, p04, p09, p12, p14, p17 are near the same exact-strip question). Replace two of them with a 2×2-table question and a "max of neither" question.
- p13 is the only three-group question and is not taught — teach it or remove it.
- Good: practice uses the taught methods (sum − total, smaller group, fractions → percent).

## 6. Verdict

- **Weak:** will solve exact two-group questions with the strip; will be misled by the "at most → max" rule on questions about "only" or "neither".
- **Medium:** will be reliable on standard range and exact questions; will lack the 2×2 table and union/neither ranges.
- **Strong:** will be fast; will meet exam three-group and two-way-table questions without a method.

**Top 3 changes**
1. Fix the "at most / at least" board so it applies to the quantity asked, and add the union / neither / "A only" range rules.
2. Add a 2×2 table slide with 2 practice questions, and teach (or remove) three-group p13 with a full solution.
3. Fix the errors: "max(8, 7)" → "min(8, 7)"; Q8 "different butterflies" wording; "Twenty−one" display bug.
