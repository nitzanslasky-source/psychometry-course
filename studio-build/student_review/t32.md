### Q 2 (geo32-g046): The diagonals of rectangle ABCD intersect at E. Given: \(CD=3\) cm and angle BEC is 120°. What is the area of the rectangle (in cm²)?

### Q 4 (geo32-g049): ABCD is a parallelogram, and \(AE\perp BC\). Given: \(AD=17\) cm, \(CD=13\) cm and \(EC=12\) cm. What is the area of the parallelogram (in cm²)?
  [FIGURE: fig/geo32-g049.png - A parallelogram with a perpendicular height]
  (1) \(221\)
  (2) \(102\)
  (3) \(204\)
  (4) \(156\)
  correct: 2
  solution: BC=17 and AB=13. Thus BE=17−12=5. In right triangle ABE, the hypotenuse 13 and leg 5 give AE=12. The area is 17×12=204.

### Q 8 (geo32-g057): The accompanying figure shows quadrilateral ABCD. Given: angle A is 112° and angle B is 104°. Which of the following cannot be the value of α?
  [FIGURE: fig/geo32-g057.png - Two angles of a general quadrilateral are given]
  (1) \(144°\)
  (2) \(16°\)
  (3) \(64°\)
  (4) \(128°\)
  correct: 0
  solution: They sum to 360°−112°−104°=144°. Each is positive, so α must be strictly less than 144°. The choice 144° would force the other angle to 0°.

### Q 9 (geo32-g058): ABCD is a parallelogram, and E lies on AD. Given: \(AB=7\) cm, \(AE=3\) cm, angle ABC is 64° and angle BCE is 58°. What is the perimeter of the parallelogram (in cm)?
  [FIGURE: fig/geo32-g058.png - An angle bisector reveals an equal side]
  (1) \(28\)
  (2) \(40\)
  (3) \(20\)
  (4) \(34\)
  correct: 3
  solution: Angle C is 116°, so angle ECD=58°. Angle D is 64°, leaving angle CED=58°. Thus ED=CD=AB=7. Then AD=3+7=10 and the perimeter is 2(7+10)=34.

### Q 10 (geo32-g059): The diagonals of rectangle ABCD intersect at E. Given: \(BC=12\) cm, and the perimeter of triangle EBC is 25 cm. What is the area of triangle ABE (in cm²)?
  [FIGURE: fig/geo32-g059.png - Four equal-area triangles inside a rectangle]
  (1) \(60\)
  (2) \(7.5\)
  (3) \(15\)
  (4) \(30\)
  correct: 2
  solution: EB=EC and their sum is 25−12=13, so each half-diagonal is 6.5 and AC=13. The rectangle’s other side is √(13²−12²)=5. The diagonals divide its area 60 into four equal areas, so ABE has area 15.

### Q 11 (geo32-g060): ABCD is a rhombus. Given: \(BD=6\) cm and angle ABD is 60°. What is the area of the rhombus (in cm²)?
  [FIGURE: fig/geo32-g060.png - A rhombus made from two equilateral triangles]
  (1) \(9\sqrt3\)
  (2) \(36\sqrt3\)
  (3) \(36\)

  correct: 3
  solution: AB=AD, so triangle ABD is isosceles. Its 60° base angle makes it equilateral, with side 6. All rhombus sides are 6, so BCD is equilateral too. Twice 6²√3 divided by 4 gives 18√3.

### Q 12 (geo32-g061): ABCD is a square, and O is a point inside it such that triangle AOD is equilateral. What is angle OBC?
