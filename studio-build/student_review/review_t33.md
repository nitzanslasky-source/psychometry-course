## 1. Coverage — what is missing

Most important first.

- **Chord rules are never taught, but practice uses them.** p22 says "the perpendicular from the center bisects the chord". adv-p21 needs the same rule. Q14 also uses it quietly ("height = median"). This is one of the most common NITE circle facts. Add one slide after "Equal chords": "Radius ⊥ chord ⇔ it cuts the chord in half. Then use the right triangle r, d, ½ chord." Add one guided question (radius 13, distance 5 → chord 24).
- **Tangent lengths in an inscribed circle (incircle).** p26 (inradius of a 6-8-10 triangle) and adv-p15 (the sums trick) need "tangent segments from each vertex are equal". The lesson only shows this as a kite. Add a slide: a triangle with its incircle, equal tangent pieces marked x, y, z. Add the shortcut: in a right triangle, r = (a + b − c) : 2. Also: a quadrilateral drawn around a circle has AB + CD = AD + BC. p26 has **no figure**; it needs one.
- **Scaling.** "Radius ×k → circumference ×k, area ×k²" (p24: +20% → +44%). This is not taught here. The same rule is behind the Nested Shapes slide ("√2 for sides means 2 for areas"). One slide: "double r → C ×2, S ×4".
- **Distance between centres.** Q13 says "tangent circles → sum of radii". It never says: internally tangent → R − r. Add both on one board.
- **Segment area and two overlapping circles.** "Segment = sector − triangle" is not named. p27 (the lens) and adv-p23 (the gap) need it. Also: two equal circles, each through the other's centre → equilateral triangles, 120°. Add one line to the memory card.
- **Common external tangent of two circles** (adv-p20). This needs the right-trapezoid trick (drop a perpendicular, R − r, Pythagoras). It is not taught. It appears on NITE. Add a guided example.
- **Right triangle in a circle.** Its circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse. A rectangle's diagonal is a diameter (adv-p25). Q11 covers half of this. Say the rectangle case on the board.
- **Smaller gaps:** revolutions of a wheel = distance ÷ circumference, with a unit check (adv-p22, m → cm). A regular hexagon in a circle has side = r (this can go in T34, but point to it here).

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- Q1 is the first question after the rules, and it is the reflex-angle trap. That is good. But Method 3 ("the base angles… add up to angle ABC, twice over. So 112 is 360 minus twice the angle") is far too compressed. Cut it, or show it step by step on the board.
- Slide "Equal chords" has **no figure**. Its proof uses congruent triangles with SSS, which is not taught in T31. Add a figure. Keep only the rule and "join the ends to O".
- The names "golden / silver triangle" are used (Q8, Q9, Q14, Q15) with no reminder of the ratios. Add a small box on the first use: silver 1 : 1 : √2, golden 1 : √3 : 2.
- "Pavlov" (Q2) is a cultural reference. Some students will not know it. "Automatic — every time" is enough.
- Q20 estimate: "shorter than the square around the circle" is confusing. Better: "circle area < area of the square around it (36) → 6·BC < 36 → BC < 6."

**Medium student**
- The sector and arc lesson ("which fraction of the circle") is very good. The memory card table of angles is excellent.
- Q18 has lots of lines. The text never says where B and C are. Add: "B and C lie on the arc AD that does not contain F."
- Q16 Method 3 says to write "1+" on each of the three arcs. That is only true if the arc is split into three 60° pieces. Simpler and correct: "the curved part is πr ≈ 3.14r".
- The Nested Shapes triangle case ("outer = 4 × inner") is stated without a picture of why. Show the inner triangle turned upside down: the outer triangle is made of 4 copies.

**Strong student**
- Good: eliminating by partial calculation (Q14, Q15), plugging in r = 2 when r = 1 does not separate the choices (Q16), and "a ratio of two perimeters can't contain r".
- Missing hard traps: the common tangent (adv-p20), the lens (p27), the incircle (p26). All three appear only in practice, without teaching. See section 1.
- Add the ring shortcut: a chord of the big circle that is tangent to the small circle, length 2h, gives ring area = πh². It solves adv-p21-type ring questions in seconds.

## 3. Correctness

**No wrong keys and no mathematical errors.** Text problems:
- **adv-p06 solution:** "angle ABD is half of that:2p+3q:2". This reads as (2p + 3q) : 2, which is wrong. It should be 2p + 3q/2. The ":" used as a division sign causes this. Across the solutions ("3π:2", "πr(1−α:90)", "16π:8", "135:360=3:8") use proper fractions in the English course.
- **p16:** "A kite with opposite angles of 90° and 72°" is ambiguous, because a kite has one pair of equal opposite angles. Write: "a kite whose two unequal opposite angles are 90° and 72°".
- Missing spaces in solutions: Q9 "radii,8"; Q15 "a30°–60°–90°"; Q16 "totaling(π+2)r", "obtain(π+2):3"; Q21 "expand:6r+9"; p24 "Thus(R−r)"; adv-p17 "area,36"; adv-p23 "a60° sector". Also in figure captions: "A150-degree", "a45-degree", "a2-to-1".
- On-screen text shows a minus sign instead of a hyphen: Q5 "non−overlapping", Q12 "α−sector".

## 6. Verdict

- **Weak:** yes for angles, area, circumference and sectors. Will get stuck on chords, the incircle and the common tangent, and on Q1 Method 3.
- **Medium:** yes for most exam circle questions, if the chord rule and scaling are added.
- **Strong:** well served with shortcuts. Still needs the untaught hard patterns (common tangent, lens, incircle) to be taught, not only met in practice.

**Top 3 changes:**
1. Add a short lesson with "chord and tangent lengths": the perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord; equal tangent segments in an incircle (r = (a+b−c)/2); the common external tangent. Add one guided question for each. This covers the 8 practice questions that now have no teaching.
2. Fix the figures that give away or mislead: Q6 "12", p05 and p15 (AC drawn through O), Q15 radius already drawn, Q12 inner circle, p20 double "O".
3. Add scaling (area ×k²) and the segment and lens formula to the lesson and memory cards. Fix the ":" division notation (adv-p06 reads wrong) and the missing spaces.
