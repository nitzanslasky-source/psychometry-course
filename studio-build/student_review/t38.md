### Q 1 (geo38-g173): An equilateral triangle, a square, a regular hexagon and a regular octagon each have perimeter 24 cm. Which has the greatest area?
  (1) The square
  (2) The regular hexagon
  (3) The regular octagon
  (4) The equilateral triangle
  correct: 2
  solution: All four shapes are regular and have equal perimeter. Among regular polygons under that condition, the one with the most sides has the greatest area: the octagon.

### Q 2 (geo38-g174): Two convex hexagonal garden plots have the same area, 90 m². One is regular and the other is not regular. Which statement is correct?
  (1) The perimeters are equal.
  (2) It cannot be determined from the information given.
  (3) The nonregular hexagon has the greater perimeter.
  (4) The regular hexagon has the greater perimeter.
  correct: 2
  solution: Among hexagons of a specified area, the regular hexagon has the least perimeter, with equality only for a regular hexagon. The nonregular hexagon therefore has a strictly greater perimeter.

### Q 3 (geo38-g176): A triangle has side lengths 9 cm, 12 cm and 16 cm. Angle α is opposite the 16-cm side. Which of the following statements is necessarily true?
  [FIGURE: fig/geo38-g176.png - Triangle ABC]
  (1) It cannot be determined from the information given.
  (2) \(\alpha>90°\)
  (3) \(\alpha<90°\)
  (4) \(\alpha=90°\)
  correct: 1
  solution: The sides 9 and 12 would meet at 90° if the opposite side were 15. The actual opposite side is 16, so the included angle α is greater than 90°.

### Q 4 (geo38-g178): PQRS is a trapezoid with PS ∥ QR. T lies on QR, and ST ⟂ QR. Which of the following statements is necessarily true?
  [FIGURE: fig/geo38-g178.png - A trapezoid split by a perpendicular from an upper vertex]
  (1) The area of triangle STR is greater than the area of trapezoid PQTS.
  (2) The area of triangle STR is less than the area of trapezoid PQTS.
  (3) The area of triangle STR equals the area of trapezoid PQTS.
  (4) None of the above is necessarily true.
  correct: 3
  solution: Both regions have height ST. Their areas are \(\frac12TR\times ST\) and \(\frac12(PS+QT)\times ST\). The question gives no comparison between TR and PS+QT, so all three area relationships are possible.

### Q 5 (geo38-g179): AB is a diameter of a circle, and chord CD is perpendicular to AB. The four regions are labeled I, II, III and IV, as shown in the accompanying figure. Ava claims I = II in area. Noah claims II = III. Mia claims I + III = II + IV. Which of the claims are necessarily true?
  [FIGURE: fig/geo38-g179.png - A horizontal diameter and a perpendicular chord divide a disk into four regions]
  (1) Noah’s and Mia’s only
  (2) All three
  (3) Ava’s and Mia’s only
  (4) Ava’s only
  correct: 2
  solution: Reflection across AB maps region I onto II and IV onto III. Thus I = II and III = IV. Adding gives I+III = II+IV. Region II need not equal III because CD need not be a diameter.

  solution: The diameter is 8 cm and is the longest chord. Since AB is not a diameter, AB < 8. Thus x+2.5 < 8 and x < 5.5.

### Q None (geo38-core-p21): In the same circle, chords AB and CD are at perpendicular distances 1 cm and 3 cm from the center, respectively. Which of the following statements is necessarily true?

  (2) It cannot be determined from the information given.
  (3) \(AB>CD\)
  (4) \(AB<CD\)

### Q None (geo38-core-p23): Two sides of a triangle have lengths 5 cm and 7 cm. The third side has an integer length. How many different lengths can it have?
  (1) \(8\)
  (2) \(10\)

### Q None (geo38-core-p24): A, B, P and Q lie on one circle. P and Q lie on the same side of chord AB and are different from its endpoints. Which of the following is necessarily true?
  [FIGURE: fig/geo38-core-p24.png - Two inscribed angles subtending the same chord from the same side]
  (1) \(\angle APB>\angle AQB\)
  (2) \(\angle APB<\angle AQB\)
  (3) It cannot be determined from the information given.
  (4) \(\angle APB=\angle AQB\)
  correct: 3
  solution: Both angles subtend the same arc AB opposite their vertices. Each equals half that arc’s measure, so they are equal.

### Q None (geo38-core-p25): A triangle has side lengths 7 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm. Which statement is correct?
  (1) The triangle has an obtuse angle.
  (2) The triangle is isosceles.
  (3) All three angles are acute.
  (4) The triangle has a right angle.
  correct: 2
  solution: The largest angle is opposite 10. Since 10² = 100 < 7²+8² = 113, even this largest angle is acute. Therefore all three are acute.

### Q None (geo38-core-p26): Four distinct vertical straight lines and three distinct horizontal straight lines are drawn in one plane. How many intersection points are formed?
  (1) \(12\)
  (2) \(7\)
  (3) \(10\)
  (4) \(14\)
  correct: 0
  solution: Each of the 4 vertical lines meets all 3 horizontal lines once, giving 4×3 = 12 distinct intersections. Lines within the same family are parallel and do not meet.

### Q None (geo38-core-p27): Two disks each have radius 3 cm, and their centers are 4 cm apart. Let A be the area covered by at least one disk. Which of the following statements is necessarily true?
  [FIGURE: fig/geo38-core-p27.png - Two overlapping equal disks with distinct centers]
  (1) \(A\leq9\pi\)
  (2) \(A>18\pi\)
  (3) \(9\pi<A<18\pi\)
  (4) \(A=18\pi\)
  correct: 2
  solution: The center distance 4 is less than the sum of radii 6, so the disks overlap in positive area. Their union is therefore less than the sum 18π. Their centers differ and neither equal disk contains the other, so their union is greater than one disk’s area 9π.
