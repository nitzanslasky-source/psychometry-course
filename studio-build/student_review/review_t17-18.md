## Topic 17: The Number Line

**Depends on:** negative and fractional exponents, roots as powers (T8–T11); odd roots of negatives exist (T9 slide on even/odd roots); inequality moves, adding inequalities, "a minus flips it" (T12); absolute value as distance (T13, used in q-506 and the distance/midpoint extras); comparing simple fractions (T2–T3).
**Needed by:** T20 (Algebraic understanding), T21 (Trial, limits and patterns), T37 (coordinate system: distance and midpoint on an axis).

### Most important points
1. **The two lessons contradict each other on negative exponents.** Lesson 1, slide 6 and the recap say: "Exceptional powers first: even power of a negative, **negative exponent**" (flip it first). Lesson 2, slide 7 lists only the even power, and says: "a negative exponent? … keep it as a power of minus one. **No need to flip.**" The memory card again says "a negative exponent flips the base". A weak student does not know which rule to use. Pick one message: "With the arrows, keep negative exponents as they are. Without arrows (hierarchy / same operation), flip first." Say it in both lessons and on the card.
2. **The most common NITE form is missing: a picture of a number line with letters on it.** For example: points a, b, 0, c, 1 marked (sometimes "the drawing is not to scale"), then "Which of these is necessarily positive / the largest / could equal b·c?" There is no such question in the topic. Add one slide ("Read the picture: which range is each letter in? Is the scale trustworthy?") and 3–4 questions.
3. **Q8 (q-500), Method 2, teaches a trick that is not valid.** "Replace every ten with a two." It changes the order of the choices: with x = 1/4, 2x = √x (both 1/2), but 10x = 2.5 and ¹⁰√x ≈ 0.87. It only works here because choice 4 is far ahead of the others. Remove the trick, or add: "Only safe when you need one clear winner. Check it."
4. **Distance and midpoint are tested but never taught.** Extras t17-3-3 (distance −8 to 5) and t17-3-6 (midpoint) have no lesson. Add one slide: distance = bigger − smaller = |a − b|; midpoint = (a + b) : 2; "the point one third of the way from a to b".
5. **Multiplying or dividing by a negative number is not taught.** Slide 4 ("Multiply & divide") covers positive multipliers only. The "mirror" idea is about a negative number times a positive one. Q5 and q-511 need "negative × fraction". Add one row: "× a negative → jump to the other side of zero; the order flips" (link to T12 "a minus flips it").

### 1. Coverage: missing or only mentioned in passing
- Number line drawings with letters, with or without scale (see point 2).
- **The full table per range for x, −x, 1/x, x², √x.** Slide 7 mentions "reciprocals can flip the order" in one sentence only. Extra t17-3-4 (1/x for 2 < x < 5) needs "reciprocals reverse the order of positive numbers". Teach it.
- "Necessarily / could be / not necessarily": Q4 and Q9 use it; no slide says how to test each (one example vs one counter-example).
- Border values −1, 0, 1 as traps: said once (slide 3), never tested.
- Same range, different powers (2³⁰ vs 3²⁰, √2 vs ∛3): not here. Cross-reference T10/T11, or it is a gap.

### 2. Teaching quality per level
- **Weak:**
  - Lesson 2 uses "odd power" for 1/3, 1/5 and −5 (Q2 video, Q7 video: "Fifth root — an odd root. Minus five — odd"). "Odd" is never defined for fractions or negative exponents. Say: "For a negative base, the arrow rule works for odd powers, odd roots, and their negatives (−1, −3, 1/3, 1/5)."
  - Slide 5 ("Hierarchy"): "ALWAYS bigger — whatever power or root" holds only for positive powers (odd for negatives). Write that condition on the board.
  - Slide 7 / memory card: "Same operation? Ignore it." The card has no warning. (−3)² vs (−2)² breaks it. Put "positive numbers only" on the card.
- **Medium:** Two methods per question is good. But written solutions (Q2, Q3, Q5, Q7, Q8) use decimals like −0.794, −0.871 that nobody can compute on the exam. Use nice numbers, as the videos do.
- **Strong:** Q1 and Q2 are slow. Needs the four-number test kit (see 4), picture questions and "could be" traps. Q7 Method 3 is a good fast idea.

### 3. Correctness
- All keys are correct (Q1–Q9, q-502–q-511, all 7 extras).
- **Q3 (q-495) written solution, wrong reason:** "a linear factor b that is no larger than the linear factors in the competing expressions". In a·b² the single factor is a, which is smaller than b. Better: "b·a² = ab·a and a·b² = ab·b; a < b, so b·a² is smaller. The others have c or b³."
- **Q8 (q-500) Method 2**: invalid trick (point 3).
- **Board text looks broken** on lesson 1, slide 2 and slide 9, and lesson 2, slide 8: `$x<-1 \qquad -11$`. The middle ranges ("−1 < x < 0 · 0 < x < 1 · x > 1") are missing. Check that the real slides show all four ranges.
- q-504 (gap a − b) is correct but off-topic. Move it to T12 or T20.

### 4. Methods and tricks to add or teach differently
- **Four-number test kit** (slide after "Four ranges"): "Range not given? Test 2, 1/2, −1/2, −2 (and 0, 1 if allowed)." Fastest safe method for "necessarily" and "in which range" questions (Q6, q-509).
- **Picture questions** (point 2): write each letter's range under the drawing, then use the arrows.
- **Reciprocal row** in the table: 1/x for each range (x > 1 → between 0 and 1; 0 < x < 1 → above 1; −1 < x < 0 → below −1; x < −1 → between −1 and 0).
- **Nice numbers for roots:** x = 1/4 for √, ±1/8 for ∛, −1/32 for ⁵√ (as in the Q7 video). Put it on the card.
- **Counter-example hunting** for "not necessarily": push values to the edges (as in the Q4 video). Name it in the lesson.

### 5. Practice

- Repetitive: q-505 = Q3 turned around; q-508 ≈ Q2; q-509 ≈ Q6; q-502 and q-507 both test "b/a > 1".
- Too easy at the end: extras 1, 3, 5, 6 are one-step.
- Missing hard items: a picture question; a "could be" item with a border trap; a question mixing ranges (x < −1 < y < 0: is xy > 1?).

### 6. Verdict
- **Weak:** Can do direct "which is largest" with plugging in. Will be confused by the negative-exponent rule and "odd" roots. Not ready for picture questions.
- **Medium:** Will solve most text questions. Lacks picture questions and the reciprocal row.
- **Strong:** Fast with arrows. Will meet exam-hard picture and "could be" questions without training.

**Top 3 changes**
1. Add a "picture of the number line" slide and 3–4 questions (letters placed on an axis, with and without scale).
2. Make the negative-exponent rule the same in both lessons and on the card. Define "odd" for roots and negative exponents.
3. Remove or fix the "replace 10 with 2" trick in Q8. Add a short slide on reciprocals, negative multipliers, distance and midpoint (these are already tested in the extras).

## Topic 18: Exercises with Letters

**Depends on:** place value and column addition with carries (T1); divisibility rules for 3, 9, 11 (T15); remainders (T15, q-537); primes (T14, Q8 and q-538); simple linear equations (T6–T7, for the algebraic form); counting (T28, extra t18-3-4); ratio (extra t18-3-2).
**Needed by:** T19 (Defining a new operation, q-571 uses digits), T21 (Trial, limits and patterns), T22 (word problems with digits), T28 (counting numbers with digit rules).

### Most important points
1. **Carries are not taught, and there is a trap.** Slide 4 says "B plus something equals B — that must be zero." True only in the **ones** column. In any other column a carry of 1 can come in, and then "A + B + 1 ends in A" means **B = 9**. Add one slide: "The carry from two numbers is 0 or 1. From three or four numbers it can be 2 or 3. In a middle column: X + Y ends in X → Y = 0 or Y = 9."
2. **Most written solutions in the practice set use heavy algebra, not the method taught.** q-522, q-525, q-530, q-533, q-534, q-540 solve with equations like "9A = 19B − 97". The lesson says (slide 9): "plugging in two numbers is faster". A weak student cannot follow these solutions. Add a short column-method solution to each (example for q-540: flip to BA + B3 = 1AB; ones: A + 3 ends in B; tens: 2B + carry = 10 + A; try B = 7).
3. **Many facts used in questions are not in the lesson or card:**
   - AA = 11A and AAA = 111A = 3·37·A (Q5, Q7, q-539, extra t18-3-5).
   - ABC − CBA = 99(A − C) (q-532).
   - Only the ones digits decide the ones digit of a product (extra t18-3-3).
   - Largest / smallest number with a given digit sum (q-529).
   Add them to the "Facts to know" card and one slide.
4. **q-532 solution shows a visible mistake to students:** "(Example: 621 and 226? No — use H−U=4 with same tens: 521 and 125 …)". Delete "621 and 226? No —".
5. **Notation is confusing for a weak student.** Slide 3 uses a bar (AB with a line over it) and says "It is NOT A times B". The question stems have no bar, and Q2 writes "BA · A", with "·" meaning times. Use the bar everywhere, or add in every stem "(AB is a two-digit number)".

### 1. Coverage
- Carries (point 1).
- Number of digits in a **product** (for example 3-digit × 2-digit = 4 or 5 digits). Sums are covered (q-521, q-527); products are not.
- Ones digit of a power (cycles 2, 4, 8, 6 …). Only extra t15-3-2 touches it. If no topic teaches cycles, add it here or in T15.
- Digit-sum divisibility (3, 9) inside letter puzzles: taught in T15. A cross-reference line would help.

### 2. Teaching quality per level
- **Weak:**
  - Slide 4 goes too fast: "Notice the carry" is one sentence. Show the carry written above the column.
  - Slide 5: "a sum with more digits has leading digit 1" is true for **two** numbers. q-527 shows four numbers can give 2 or 3. Say "for two numbers" on the board.
  - Q7 video: "With a five here we'd use the five-rule. Here it's a three" is confusing. Delete the first half.
  - Q9 video, Method 2: the "cube sits on the tens digit" sentence is unclear, and the plug-in still uses the algebra. Plug in directly: T = 3 → 30 + U = U³ + 3 + U → U³ = 27.
- **Medium:** The four steps are clear; Q1–Q8 follow them. The gap is the practice solutions (point 2).
- **Strong:** Slide 9 says "plugging in is faster most of the time". But the algebraic form is faster in q-528, q-531, q-539. Say when to choose it: "'always' questions with no result → algebraic form."

### 3. Correctness
- All keys are correct (Q1–Q9, q-521–q-540, all 7 extras).
- **q-532:** visible self-correction in the solution (point 4).
- **q-536 solution, wrong reason:** "Any BB ≥ 22 makes these products four-digit." False: 33 × 22 = 726 and 11 × 22 = 242 are three-digit. The answer (33) is still right. Fix: "For 55, 66, 77, even BB = 22 gives four digits (55 × 22 = 1210). For 33 × 22 = 726, the first and last digits differ."
- **q-522 solution:** "For 5A to end the way 25 + 2B does, 2B must be a multiple of 5" is hard to read. Easier: "ones column: 2B ends in 0 → B = 5".
- **Slide 7:** plugging in with 51 and 52 works here. But the method needs a warning: with 51 + 15 = 66 and 62 + 26 = 88, choice "2" would survive both tries. Tell students to pick test numbers of different types (one with an even result, one with an odd result).
- **Wording:** Q6 "whose hundreds digit is literally 6" (remove "literally"); Q1 stem "each letter being the digit in that position" is long. Suggest the standard form: "A, B, C and D represent digits. AB and CB are two-digit numbers, and DDB is a three-digit number."

### 4. Methods and tricks to add or teach differently
- **Carry slide** (point 1), right after slide 4.
- **Estimate the size first** as a named step (Q6 Method 1 does it: 24² = 576, 27² = 729). Put it into the 4 steps: "3 · Leftmost digit / size".
- **Plug in the answers** as a named method. Q8 does it well. Say in the lesson: "When they ask for one letter, try the choices, starting from the middle."
- **Repdigit and reversal facts** on the card: AA = 11A, AAA = 111A = 3·37·A, AB ± BA, ABC − CBA = 99(A − C).

### 5. Practice
- 9 guided + 20 practice + 7 extras. Plenty.
- Off-topic: extra t18-3-4 (counting, T28), q-537 (remainders, T15), q-538 (primes, T14), extra t18-3-2 (ratio). These are fine as mixed review only if those topics come first. Otherwise move them.
- Repetitive: q-524, q-531, extra 1 and slide 7 are all "AB ± BA". q-523 and q-526 are both "consecutive digits".
- Spread: good. The hard ones (q-536, q-537, q-540) are exam-hard. Missing: one puzzle with a middle-column carry trap (Y = 9), and one product-size question.

### 6. Verdict
- **Weak:** Can do simple addition puzzles. Will be lost with carries and with the algebra in the practice solutions.
- **Medium:** Ready for most exam items after the carry slide. Will still need the repdigit facts.
- **Strong:** Ready. Wants the algebraic form presented as an equal route, and harder carry traps.

**Top 3 changes**
1. Add a carry slide (carry 0 or 1; "ends in X" → 0 or 9 in a middle column; more than two numbers → bigger carry).
2. Rewrite the practice solutions that use heavy algebra with a short column / plug-in solution. Fix q-532 and q-536 text.
3. Add the missing facts (AA = 11A, AAA = 111A = 3·37·A, ABC − CBA = 99(A − C), ones digit of a product, product digit count) to the lesson and card. Use one notation (bar) in every stem.
