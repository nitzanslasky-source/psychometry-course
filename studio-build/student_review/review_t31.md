## 1. Coverage — what is missing

- **Acute / right / obtuse from the sides (c² vs a² + b²).** This is not taught, but two solutions use it: Q16 ("3.75, 3.75, 6 provides an obtuse example") and adv-p18 ("8² > 5² + 5² makes the angle obtuse"). It is also a known NITE item. Add one board line after the Pythagoras slide "Right triangles only": "c² = a² + b² → right; c² > a² + b² → obtuse; c² < a² + b² → acute (c = longest side)."
- **Area two ways (altitude to the hypotenuse).** adv-p17 needs "leg × leg = hypotenuse × altitude". It is not taught. Add one slide after "Any side as base".
- **Largest possible area.** A classic trap: "Two sides are 6 and 8. Which could be the area?" The area is at most 24, and it reaches 24 only at a right angle. It is missing. Add one guided question.
- **Equal angles → equal sides (the converse).** This is used in p03, Q17 and adv-p07, but it is said only once, inside Q17's video. Put it on the "Isosceles triangle" board and the memory card: "base angles equal ⇔ legs equal".
- **Angle bisector** is used from Q4 on, but the "Triangles" lesson lists only the median and the altitude. It is taught in T30, so just add it as the third special line in slides 2–5.
- **Counting integer third sides.** p22 and adv-p24 train it, but no shortcut is taught (see section 4).

## 2. Teaching quality per level

**Weak student**
- **Order problem: Pythagoras is used before it is taught.** The "Equilateral Triangle Area" video says "we use the Pythagorean theorem — we'll learn it properly in a moment". Q7 depends on it too. Both come before "The Pythagorean Theorem". Move the equilateral-area video (and Q7) to after the 30-60-90 lesson. There h = a√3/2 comes out for free: it is the long leg of a golden triangle with short leg a/2.
- **Slide "Exterior angle": γ now means a different angle.** On the slide before, γ was the interior angle at C. Now the exterior angle is also called γ. Also, fig v48 is the angle-sum figure again: it still has the parallel line through C and the interior arc at C. The extension of AC will be drawn right next to that parallel line. Use a clean figure with the extension, and call the exterior angle δ.
- **Slide "Between diff and sum"** writes c > a − b. A weak student may compute 7 − 12. Say "big minus small".
- **Q17 video:** "it's not very clear what to do with these numbers… skip it." Fine as tactics, but end with a quick sketch of a slanted median of 6 to a side of 4.
- **p20 has no figure,** only a long text with a triangle, a parallel line and three angles. Add a figure.

## 4. Methods and tricks to add

- **Integer third side:** the count is 2 × (shorter side) − 1. For 9 and 14: 17 (p22). Put it in the slide "Between diff and sum" or on the memory card.
- **Triple spotting from the perimeter:** adv-p21 (hypotenuse 26, legs sum 34). Test 10-24-26 first: 10 + 24 = 34, so the area is 120. This is faster than (a+b)². Add it as a second method in the solution.
- **Same base, same height → same area** (Q6, Q21, adv-p23) deserves a named memory-card line: "the vertex moves along a parallel line → the area does not change".
